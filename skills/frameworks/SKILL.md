# venture frameworks

Each framework exists to answer **one decision question** about this idea, for this founder, in this town. A framework that doesn't change a decision is padding. Skip padding.

## Rules for every framework
1. **Evidence first.** Every factual statement is a claim with an evidence label (rule 8): `[source: <url>]`, `[FOUNDER-COUNTED: …]`, `[FOUNDER-ESTIMATE: …]`, `[PEER-TOWN: …]`, `[ASSUMPTION: …]`. `[SIMULATED: …]` is used only in the customer panel and never counts as evidence.
2. **Small towns are not "no data".** Use peer-town benchmarks, the big-brand presence map, the city profile and founder-counted facts before concluding anything is unknowable. Thin online data lowers confidence. On its own it is not evidence against the idea.
3. **End with a verdict line:** `verdict: SUPPORTS | NEUTRAL | AGAINST — <one-line reason>`.
4. **Critical frameworks.** An `AGAINST` on **buyer power and substitutes** (Five Forces), **steal-ability**, **price ladder**, or **channel access** (funnel / 4Ps place) is critical. Two or more critical `AGAINST`s, or one that rests on verified evidence and cannot be fixed without changing the customer, problem or revenue model, means the strategist recommends **DROP**.
5. Builders (rule 1): look hard for the angle that works. Never inflate a verdict.

## A. Competition

### A1. Competitor map
**Question:** who already takes this customer's money for this job?
List **direct** competitors, **indirect** ones (a different product for the same job), **substitutes** (including "do nothing", DIY, family, WhatsApp groups, the informal sector), and **big national players**, each present or absent in this town. For each: name, price, what customers like, what they complain about, and evidence.
**Verdict:** SUPPORTS if paying competition exists with a clear, evidenced weakness; AGAINST if a free substitute is good enough, or a dominant player has no visible weakness.

### A2. Steal-ability
**Question:** can we take customers from them, and which ones?
For the 2–3 most important competitors:
- why their customers would switch to us (a specific pain we fix);
- the switching cost (money, effort, relationship, habit);
- the trust barrier;
- how the competitor would respond (match price, copy, lock in, ignore).

Name the **first segment we steal** and why it's reachable.
**Verdict:** AGAINST (critical) if switching costs exceed the benefit for every segment.

### A3. Price ladder
**Question:** is our price set to win?
Put every competitor's and substitute's price on one ladder, with our price on it. Add the **expected price** (what customers pay today for the job, from evidence) and the **value-based price** (the money or time saved, or the result gained). State where we sit (cheaper / same / premium) and why that's right for the segment.
**Verdict:** AGAINST (critical) if we're priced above a comparable alternative with no evidenced reason to pay more, or so low that the cautious economics can't work.

### A4. Porter's Five Forces
**Question:** is this market structurally able to give a small entrant a profit?
Rate each force LOW / MEDIUM / HIGH with evidence:
- **rivalry**;
- **threat of new entrants** (what stops the next person copying us);
- **threat of substitutes** (critical);
- **buyer power** (critical: can customers push the price to zero or walk away easily?);
- **supplier and platform power** (WhatsApp, marketplaces, app stores, payment rails, a landlord, a key partner).

**Verdict:** AGAINST if substitutes or buyer power are HIGH with no mitigation.

### A5. Blue Ocean: ERRC grid
**Question:** is our difference real or cosmetic?
Against the main competitors, list what we **Eliminate**, **Reduce**, **Raise** and **Create**. The Create and Raise items must map to a complaint or unmet need with evidence.
**Verdict:** NEUTRAL or AGAINST if the grid is only "cheaper".

## B. The customer

### B1. Jobs-to-be-Done
**Question:** what job is the customer hiring for, and what do they fire to hire us?
Cover the job statement, the functional, emotional and social dimensions, what's hired today, and the "fire" trigger (the moment they look for something better).

### B2. Need-gap analysis
**Question:** how big is the gap between what they want and what they get, and for whom?
Give the desired outcome, the current outcome, the size of the gap (time, money, quality, access), the **underserved segment**, and why the gap is open (brands absent here, too small for big players, a format nobody offers).
**Verdict:** SUPPORTS only when the gap is evidenced (complaints, peer-town under-supply, brand absence, founder-counted observation), not merely plausible.

### B3. Value Proposition Canvas
**Question:** does the offer fit the customer's pains and gains?
Map the customer's jobs, pains and gains, then our pain relievers and gain creators. Mark fit as strong, partial or none.

### B4. Kano model
**Question:** what must the first version have, and what can wait?
Sort features into **must-haves** (absent means rejected), **performance** (more is better), **delighters**, and **indifferent**. The MVP is the must-haves plus one performance feature. This prevents building the wrong thing first.

### B5. Consumer behaviour
**Question:** will these people actually change behaviour, and who moves first?
- **Adoption** (diffusion of innovations): who the innovators and early adopters are in this town, and how to reach them; whether the early majority will follow.
- **Trust:** local versus national brands, the role of a known face, guarantees, cash on delivery or pay-after.
- **Habit and inertia:** what the customer does today without thinking.
- **Price sensitivity:** income cycles (salary dates, harvests), festival and wedding seasons, cash versus UPI versus credit.
- **Decision-maker versus user:** for example, parent and child, or household head and member.
- **Risk perception:** what feels risky to buy from a newcomer.

**Verdict:** AGAINST if the change asked of the customer is large and no trust device exists.

## C. Market and strategy

### C1. STP
**Question:** exactly whom do we serve first, and how do we want to be seen?
**Segment** the market (2–4 segments with size evidence), pick the **target**, and write a one-sentence **positioning**: "for <segment> who <need>, <offer> is the <category> that <benefit>, unlike <alternative>."

### C2. TAM / SAM / SOM, bottom-up
**Question:** is the reachable market big enough to matter to this founder?
Build it bottom-up and label every step:
- **TAM:** population in the area × share with the need × annual spend;
- **SAM:** the part we can serve (geography, format, price band);
- **SOM:** what we can realistically win in 12 months, given the founder's hours and channels.

Use census or peer-town data where local data is missing. Never use national top-down market reports as local.
**Verdict:** AGAINST if the SOM at the cautious price can't reach break-even.

### C3. Ansoff matrix
**Question:** how risky is this move?
Place it in **market penetration** (existing product, existing market), **product development**, **market development** (a proven product in a new town or segment; often the best small-town play), or **diversification** (new product, new market: riskiest). Say what the placement implies for evidence needs.

### C4. PESTLE
**Question:** is there an outside force that blocks or helps this?
Cover only the factors that matter to this idea: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal (licences, consumer law, data law) and Environmental. Each needs evidence.
**Verdict:** AGAINST if a legal or regulatory blocker exists that the founder can't clear within the contract.

### C5. Marketing mix (4Ps)
**Question:** can this founder put it in front of the customer, at this price, through a channel they can reach?
- **Product:** from Kano.
- **Price:** from the price ladder.
- **Place:** where and how it's delivered.
- **Promotion:** the specific channels in this town (WhatsApp groups, local Instagram pages, shopkeepers, schools, community leaders, newspapers), with reach evidence or founder-counted group sizes.

**Verdict:** AGAINST (critical) if no channel the founder can reach gets to the target segment.

### C6. Funnel (AIDA) and conversion assumptions
**Question:** what does it really cost to win a customer?
Go **awareness → interest → decision → action** for the main channel, with a number and evidence label at each step. The result is the people reached per month and the conversion rate, which feed the economics simulation directly.

### C7. Moat
**Question:** what gets stronger with time, if anything?
Consider local trust and reputation, relationships, data, a community, switching costs, cost advantage, exclusive partners. "None yet" is an honest answer, but then say how fast a copycat could arrive.

## D. Simulated customer panel (objections only, never evidence)
**Question:** what objections will real customers raise, and does the pitch survive them?
Write 8–12 short personas drawn from the city profile and STP (age, income, situation, current solution, attitude to new or local brands). For each: their first reaction to the pitch, their main objection, and what would make them try it. Label every line `[SIMULATED]`. Use the objections to improve the positioning, the trust devices and the MVP. **The panel never counts as demand evidence**, and judges ignore it as evidence.

## E. Summary
- **Framework scorecard:** a table of every framework with its verdict.
- **3 biggest strengths, 3 biggest risks** (with framework refs).
- **What the economics should use:** price (from the price ladder), people reached per month and conversion (from the funnel), churn (from behaviour), and the evidence label for each.
- **Recommendation: CARRY or DROP**, with the reason. DROP follows rule 4 above.
