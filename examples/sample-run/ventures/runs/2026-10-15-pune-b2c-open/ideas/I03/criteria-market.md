## Criteria
### KM1
- dies unless: there is demand evidence that sellers already pay for selling used furniture or appliances through an intermediary (listing fee, commission, or buy-back dealer margin) in Pune, or in a comparable Indian metro, today.
- threshold: at least 3 distinct operators with observable paying sellers or visible transaction volume (for example paid-listing tiers, commission pricing pages, or dealer shops with dated customer reviews), and at least 1 of them in Pune. A fee of at least ₹100 per listing, or a commission of at least 10%, counts as paying.
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pricing pages, Google Maps/Justdial listings with dated reviews, app-store review counts, press reports of revenue. Pune-labeled evidence is required for at least one operator; others may be labeled (other city).
- why it matters: the revenue model charges the seller ₹199 per listing; if no seller pays anyone today, willingness to pay is untested.

### KM2
- dies unless: free substitutes (OLX, Facebook Marketplace, Quikr, society and builder WhatsApp groups) are shown to fail the seller in a way a ₹199 board would fix.
- threshold: at least 2 independent sources (complaint threads, reviews, articles) that document a seller-side failure of free channels for bulky furniture or appliances (for example no-show buyers, lowballing, pickup logistics), and a named mechanism by which a paid board with no inventory and no logistics reduces that failure.
- evidence that satisfies it: dated forum or review-site posts, consumer articles, or a documented resale venture describing why sellers left free channels. Self-selected sources are acceptable if at least 2 are independent.
- why it matters: "do nothing", giving the item to the next tenant or the landlord, and free classifieds are the real competitors; a fee is justified only if free options visibly fail.

### KM3
- dies unless: the supply flow of used furniture and appliances in Pune IT-corridor areas is large enough to reach the break-even listing volume at ₹199.
- threshold: at least 500 distinct used furniture or appliance listings per month visible across existing channels in the named areas (Hinjewadi, Wakad, Baner, Kharadi, Viman Nagar, Magarpatta). A dated manual count of a 2-week sample, scaled to a month, is acceptable. At ₹199 per listing and a 10% conversion of existing listings to paid, 500 listings gives about ₹9,950/month gross.
- evidence that satisfies it: dated screenshot-backed count of listings on OLX, Quikr, Facebook Marketplace and public Pune WhatsApp/Telegram/Facebook groups, filtered by area; or tenant-turnover figures for the named areas combined with a stated resale rate.
- why it matters: market size must be shown in the target market (Pune corridor), not inferred from national data or from the Rentomojo marketing claim.

### KM4
- dies unless: the buyer side exists, meaning incoming Pune tenants are shown to buy used furniture and appliances rather than only rent new or buy new.
- threshold: at least 1 measurable demand indicator of at least 1,000 monthly searches combined for "used/second hand furniture Pune" style queries in English, Hindi and Marathi (keyword-tool data), or at least 2 sourced data points from Indian urban renters showing a purchase share of used furniture of at least 10%.
- evidence that satisfies it: keyword-planner or third-party search volume exports, market surveys, resale-platform reports (for example secondhand furniture market reports citing India), dated and with geography labels.
- why it matters: the ₹199 fee is only collected if buyers show up; the contrarian claim "tenants would rather buy used than rent" currently rests on no data (03-ideas.md notes it is untested).

### KM5
- dies unless: the graveyard of comparable resale ventures in India does not show that sellers cannot be monetised at this price point.
- threshold: at least 3 comparable ventures identified (used furniture, appliances or local P2P resale marketplaces, India, any city, last 10 years), and among those that shut down or pivoted away from seller fees, at most 1 cites low seller monetisation, low transaction frequency, or buyer-seller trust and logistics as its main cause. If 2 or more cite it, the criterion is NOT MET unless a survivor with revenue under a paid-seller model is shown.
- evidence that satisfies it: shutdown or pivot reports from Inc42, YourStory, Entrackr, founder post-mortems, Tracxn records, each with the stated cause.
- why it matters: a no-inventory listing board with a seller fee is a long-tried model; its past failures decide whether the fee is viable.
