## Criteria

### KE1
- dies unless: a working first version (WhatsApp community or group structure, listing intake form, UPI collection of the ₹199 fee, a listing template) can be built and the first paid listing collected within the contract's capital, hours and time limits, using only skills the user has (spreadsheets, social media marketing, English/Hindi/Marathi) or can hire inside K1.
- threshold: total spend to first paid listing at most ₹40,000 of the ₹1,00,000 (K1, HARD), leaving at least ₹60,000 uncommitted as the loss ceiling (K5); at most 10 hours/week (K2, HARD, no stretch); first paid listing within 3 months of start (K6, HARD); no inventory purchased and no licence needed (K8).
- evidence that satisfies it: an itemised cost and hours plan (tools, any freelancer, payment collection, ads) with sourced prices, showing the conservative case within these limits, plus a week-by-week schedule that reaches a paid listing before month 3. Any freelancer cost counts inside K1 only (K4 unlock).
- why it matters: the contract is law. An idea that needs more capital, hours or time than the HARD limits fails regardless of merit.

### KE2
- dies unless: the founder can recruit paying sellers at a cost the ₹199 fee supports, through channels the user can run alone in 10 hours/week (Instagram, WhatsApp, society groups, offline notices), not through paid-for-by-capital volume that exceeds K1.
- threshold: blended acquisition cost per paid listing at most ₹100 (about 50% of the ₹199 fee) in the conservative case, and a plan of at most 6 recurring hours/week of seller outreach that reaches at least 100 paid listings within 6 months while staying inside K1.
- evidence that satisfies it: sourced Pune-relevant channel costs (Meta/Instagram ad CPMs or CPCs in India, society-group or notice-board access terms, cost of any flyers or QR notices), and a worked funnel (reach, enquiry, listing, payment) with conversion rates labelled as sourced or ASSUMPTION. If no Pune data exists, the criterion rests on a stated, tested assumption.
- why it matters: a two-sided listing business with a sub-₹200 ticket dies on acquisition cost and on the founder's limited hours before it dies on product.

### KE3
- dies unless: the operating model survives the policies and pricing of the platforms it depends on (WhatsApp communities/groups/Business API, Instagram, the UPI payment collection method), and does not rely on a channel whose terms can end the business without a fallback.
- threshold: written platform terms (Meta WhatsApp Business/Commerce and community policies, group size and admin limits, any rules on paid listings or commercial resale) permit running paid listings, AND a fallback channel not owned by Meta (own website or listing page, Telegram, or a no-code listing site) can be stood up for at most ₹10,000 and 1 week if the account is restricted. Any WhatsApp Business API message cost (S01 figures: about ₹0.86-1.09 per marketing message plus about 18% GST, vendor figures conflict) must be less than 10% of the ₹199 fee per listing.
- evidence that satisfies it: the current Meta policy and community-limit pages, a UPI/payment collection method that fits personal-versus-business account rules for a sole proprietor, and the cost of the fallback.
- why it matters: the entire demand side lives in groups the founder does not own on a platform that can restrict accounts or change prices.

### KE4
- dies unless: the legal and compliance load of running a paid-listing intermediary in Pune is small enough to be met before first revenue at low cost, and liability for what sellers list stays with sellers.
- threshold: total registrations and legal costs to operate at most ₹15,000 and at most 4 weeks of calendar time, covering the Maharashtra Shops and Establishments question (C18 in 01 shows conflicting sources), GST registration or exemption for listing-fee income, Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules 2020 duties if the board counts as a marketplace e-commerce entity, DPDP Act duties for collected phone numbers (S08 and C19 in 01 show no clear small-business exemption), and sellers' terms of service. No licence is required for the listed goods (K8); used electrical appliances and gas items must not create a safety-liability exposure the founder cannot disclaim or insure for at most ₹5,000/year.
- evidence that satisfies it: the text of the rules or a government or professional page for each item, giving its applicability to a sole proprietor earning listing fees, and a draft disclaimer structure showing the platform is only an intermediary.
- why it matters: the user cannot lose more than ₹1,00,000 (K5), and a consumer dispute, tax notice or data-law penalty at small scale can outweigh a ₹199-per-listing revenue.

### KE5
- dies unless: the model resists the second-order failure modes of a low-ticket matching board, namely fee bypass, incumbent response and operational creep.
- threshold: (a) the ₹199 fee is collected before the seller's contact or listing becomes visible, so a buyer and seller cannot bypass it after matching, or the plan shows at least 70% of matched listings still pay despite free copies on OLX, Facebook Marketplace, Quikr or society WhatsApp groups; (b) the model needs no pickup, delivery, escrow, refunds or inventory handling inside the 10 hours/week; (c) a documented response from named incumbents (free listing, resale or buyback by Rentomojo-type rental firms, copycat Pune groups) leaves unit economics positive in the conservative case.
- evidence that satisfies it: the listing and payment flow design, the free-listing and resale policies of OLX, Facebook Marketplace and the rental incumbents (sourced), and a case where a comparable paid-listing or local-classifieds operator in India earns revenue from listing fees (with source and geography label).
- why it matters: the generator itself flags that free classifieds likely exist (03-ideas.md, I03 note), and a ₹199 fee with no moat is trivially copied or bypassed.
