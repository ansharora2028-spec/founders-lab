### A2. Steal-ability
- Rivals that matter: (1) grammar-focused tuition teachers; (2) the bus-stand class with 30 per batch; (3) "do nothing".
- Why they would switch: the tuition teacher adds no speaking practice, so there is little to steal. This is an add-on purchase, not a swap. The bus-stand class is a real swap: smaller batch and a teacher parents know.
- Switching cost: low. Fees are monthly and there is no contract. The cost is a second evening commitment for the child [ASSUMPTION].
- Trust barrier: the founder taught at a private school for 2 years, so her name is known to some parents (contract unfair advantage). A free trial evening lowers the risk further.
- Competitor response: tuition teachers could add "speaking" within a week at no cost. The bus-stand class could split batches. Neither is likely to cut price below ₹600 [ASSUMPTION].
- First segment we steal: parents of class 8-10 children in her 4 WhatsApp groups and her former students' parents. They already pay ₹800-1,500 per subject [FOUNDER-COUNTED: FK Q9, Q11] and are reachable at near-zero cost.
- verdict: NEUTRAL (critical) — the first segment is reachable and switching cost is low. The pain being fixed is evidenced only by hearsay.

## B. The customer

### B1. Jobs-to-be-Done
- Job: "Help my child speak English confidently in front of teachers, in interviews and in the next school or college step, without a long trip or a big fee."
- Functional: speaking practice, pronunciation, sentence building.
- Emotional: parent's worry that the child will fall behind peers who go to English-medium schools or to Pune courses.
- Social: parent's status; the child's confidence in front of classmates.
- Hired today: grammar-focused tuition, school English, Pune courses (heard), YouTube and apps [FOUNDER-ESTIMATE: FK Q3, Q8].
- Fire trigger: a poor English result, a child who freezes at an oral exam or interview, or a relative's child visibly speaking well [ASSUMPTION: no evidence found].
- verdict: SUPPORTS — the job is clear and there is a plausible fire trigger. Local intensity is unmeasured, so the support is modest.

### B2. Need-gap analysis
- Desired outcome: the child speaks English comfortably. Current outcome: grammar and reading only (founder hearsay, national blogs).
- Size of gap: unmeasured. Pune trips imply willingness to spend time and money [FOUNDER-ESTIMATE: FK Q3].
- Underserved segment: class 8-10 children whose parents already pay for tuition. The kids-specific class 6-10 seller was not found in directories (weak).
- Why open: seller listings target adults, no national kids' English chain is found in Satara, and online options cost ₹2,500+. Absence of search results is weak evidence.
- Counter-evidence: peer-town count shows supply in line, not under-supply [PEER-TOWN: 01-local-market.md]. No order or review data exists for any Satara spoken-English seller.
- verdict: NEUTRAL — the gap is plausible and consistent with the founder-counted facts, but evidenced mainly by hearsay and national proxies.

### B4. Kano model
- Must-haves: a qualified, known teacher; a safe room near school; a fixed schedule; small batch; UPI and cash payment; a free trial.
- Performance: speaking minutes per child; batch size; progress message to parents.
- Delighters: a short end-of-term speech day for parents; a certificate; level tests.
- Indifferent: app, LMS, Instagram content at launch.
- MVP: must-haves plus speaking minutes per child. One batch of at most 20-25 students in class 8-10, 3 evenings, ₹600.
- verdict: NEUTRAL — it clarifies the MVP without moving the decision.

### B5. Consumer behaviour
- Adoption: early adopters are parents already paying for tuition who are in her WhatsApp groups. The early majority follows through school WhatsApp groups and word of mouth [FOUNDER-COUNTED: FK Q5].
- Trust: the local known face beats national brands for small-ticket child services. The founder's 2 years of school teaching is the trust device [contract]. A free trial and monthly payment reduce the risk of buying from a newcomer.
- Habit and inertia: parents default to tuition for school subjects and do not think of speaking as a separate purchase [ASSUMPTION].
- Price sensitivity: monthly payment around salary date, cash or UPI [FOUNDER-COUNTED: FK Q6]. Exam months (Jan-March, class 10 practicals from 2027-01-28) and Diwali or summer are slow [FOUNDER-ESTIMATE: FK Q12].
- Decision-maker versus user: the parent pays and the child attends. A teenager's reluctance to give up an evening is a drop-out risk [ASSUMPTION].
- Risk perception: parents fear weak results from a new class and a child's evening wasted. A named teacher and a trial reduce this.
- verdict: NEUTRAL — the change asked is small, and a trust device exists. Child retention is the unknown.

### C2. TAM / SAM / SOM (bottom-up)
- TAM: about 9,000-12,000 children aged 11-15 [ASSUMPTION]; class 6-10 in that range about 8,000-10,000. If 20% of households would pay for an extra English class [ASSUMPTION: no benchmark found], that is 1,600-2,000 children. At ₹600 × 12 = ₹7,200 a year, TAM is about ₹1.2-1.4 crore a year.
- SAM: class 8-10, about 6 schools within 3 km, in-person evening format. Assume 25-35% of the city's class 8-10 children [ASSUMPTION], giving about 500-800 addressable children and ₹36-58 lakh a year at ₹600.
- SOM (12 months, 12 h/week, one centre): the funnel gives 12-38 students in months 1-3 (forge ASSUMPTION), and 20-25 students in the base case. At 25 students × ₹600 = ₹15,000 a month gross, about ₹10,000 after ₹5,000 rent. If the draft Act counts per batch, three batches of 25 reach about 75 students and ₹45,000 gross. Whether it counts per batch or per centre is unresolved.
- Break-even on rent: about 8-9 students (₹5,000 ÷ ₹600) [ASSUMPTION: derived from FOUNDER-ESTIMATE FK Q7]. This is reachable at the cautious funnel (9 students at 3% of 300).
- Founder income at the base case is about ₹7,000-10,000 a month for 12 hours a week. That is a side income, not a livelihood.
- verdict: NEUTRAL — break-even on rent is reachable. The upside is small and depends on the Act's per-batch or per-centre reading.
