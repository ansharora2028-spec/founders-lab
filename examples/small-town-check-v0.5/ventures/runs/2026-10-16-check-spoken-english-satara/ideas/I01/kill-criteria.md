## Market criteria
### KM1
- dies unless: at least 2 named operators of in-person spoken-English (or English-speaking) classes for school-age children (any age span inside class 5-10) have visible paying customers in Satara city or in peer towns of Maharashtra with population 50,000 to 3,00,000, at a published or reported fee of ₹500/month or more.
- threshold: >=2 operators, each with a published fee >=₹500/month AND at least one sign of real customers (>=10 dated public reviews, or a stated enrolment of >=20 students, or dated batch/admission posts within the last 12 months). Directory listings with no reviews or orders do not count.
- evidence that satisfies it: Justdial or Google Maps pages with review counts, operator websites or social pages with fee and batch posts, fee notices, local news features. Desk research is enough; an offline count by the founder (names, batch sizes, fees seen on notices) counts as FOUNDER-COUNTED only and gives at most INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.
- why it matters: demand evidence. Today the only kids' anchors in Satara are founder hearsay (about ₹1,000/month, batches of 30) and one seller listing at ₹2,000 with no customers shown.

### KM2
- dies unless: the Satara city catchment holds enough class 6-10 students that the break-even number of paying students is a small share of them.
- threshold: >=6,000 students enrolled in classes 6-10 in schools in Satara city (all managements), so that 30 paying students is at most 0.5% of them. If only the 6 schools within 3 km can be counted, >=2,500 students in classes 6-10 in those schools.
- evidence that satisfies it: UDISE+ school-wise enrolment, Satara Municipal Council or Zilla Parishad education office data, school-published strength, or the founder's counted enrolment of the 6 nearby schools.
- why it matters: market size in the target market. The only size figure now is an unverified assumption of 9,000-12,000 children aged 11-15 derived from population.

### KM3
- dies unless: the addressable age group stays large enough under the Maharashtra private-coaching rules that are in force or most likely to be in force when batches start.
- threshold: either (a) the enacted Act, or the draft text, excludes language or spoken-English classes, or drops or lowers the under-13 bar, or (b) if the under-13 bar applies, class 8-10 enrolment in the Satara catchment is still >=3,600 (the 6,000 of KM2 less roughly 40% for class 6-7), and batches of 25 or fewer remain outside registration.
- evidence that satisfies it: official text of the Maharashtra Private Coaching Centers (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2026 (draft or enacted) from the state gazette or School Education Department, with the definition of "coaching" and the age rule; enrolment from the KM2 sources.
- why it matters: timing and market access. The draft bars under-13s, and class 6-7 pupils are mostly 11-13, so a third to two fifths of the stated customer group may be illegal to enrol.

### KM4
- dies unless: in-person kids' English-speaking or skills classes in small towns have survived long enough to show repeat paying demand, and the graveyard does not show them closing for lack of paying parents.
- threshold: >=2 in-person kids' spoken-English or English-skills operators in Satara city or peer towns (Maharashtra, population 50,000 to 3,00,000) operating >=24 months with dated evidence, AND no more than 1 documented closure in those towns attributed to weak enrolment or non-payment.
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pages or reviews with dated posts across 2+ years, Wayback Machine snapshots, Justdial or Maps review history, local news or founder-counted names of closed classes with reason.
- why it matters: graveyard and timing. No Satara launch or shutdown of a spoken-English business was found, so survival and failure rates are unknown.

### KM5
- dies unless: official data shows parents of secondary-level students in urban Maharashtra already spend on private coaching at a level that makes ₹600/month (₹7,200/year) plausible as an add-on.
- threshold: Maharashtra urban share of students at upper-primary/secondary level taking private coaching >=25%, AND a reported average annual coaching spend per coaching student at that level >=₹6,000 (or per-month equivalent >=₹500).
- evidence that satisfies it: NSS 80th round Comprehensive Modular Survey: Education (2025) tables for Maharashtra (urban, by level), from MoSPI or an official report; not secondary news summaries of national averages.
- why it matters: existing spending on a substitute. The national figures cited so far (about 27% take coaching; average annual spend ₹3,988 urban across all levels) sit below ₹7,200/year, so the price may be above the typical add-on spend.

## Execution criteria
### KE1
- dies unless: the conservative case delivers a working first batch and the first paid fee within 2 months (K6, HARD), with total cash at risk (setup + room deposit + rent and materials for every month until break-even, run at the conservative enrolment) at or below ₹75,000 (K1, K5, HARD).
- threshold: setup plus deposit at most ₹25,000; cumulative cash out at worst point at most ₹75,000; first fee collected by 2026-12-16 (month 2).
- evidence that satisfies it: economics showing the cumulative cash curve at conservative enrolment (about 10 students per batch, not the 20-75 base or ceiling cases), with a sourced or founder-counted room rent and deposit (typically 3-6 months of rent in Maharashtra, needs a source), plus printed material and Instagram costs.
- why it matters: a room at ₹4,000-6,000/month gives a break-even of 8-10 students, and a deposit plus slow ramp-up could consume the capital before revenue.

### KE2
- dies unless: the format can be run lawfully on the date of launch and when the draft Maharashtra Private Coaching Centers Act, 2026 becomes final. This means either (a) spoken English for school children is outside the Act's scope, or (b) the format still works when kept to 25 or fewer students per batch and with students aged 13 or over, and the founder is not barred as a serving teacher at a recognised school.
- threshold: the official draft or final text confirms the scope, the age floor and the student threshold. In case (b), the conservative economics still hold with class 6 and most class 7 pupils (under 13) excluded and every batch at 25 or fewer. The cost of any registration needed (Shops and Establishment, municipal licence, fire or safety certificate for the room) fits inside the KE1 budget.
- evidence that satisfies it: the draft or notified Act text on the Maharashtra government site or the gazette; the Satara Collector or education office; Shops and Establishments registration rules for a one-room class. Desk research can settle this.
- why it matters: the draft carries fines of ₹1 lakh to ₹50 lakh. It may bar under-13s, which is the youngest part of the class 6-10 target. It may also bar teachers currently employed at recognised schools.

### KE3
- dies unless: the unit economics stay positive if the local incumbents respond. The bus-stand class (about ₹1,000, batches of 30, heard) could cut to ₹600 or split its batches. A tuition teacher could add a "speaking hour". PlanetSpark, SpeakX or a PW Pathshala add-on could bundle speaking practice for the same age group.
- threshold: with price held at ₹600 and no price premium over the nearest in-person incumbent, the founder still reaches at least 10 paying students per month across batches by month 2 and keeps them for at least 3 months. Break-even stays at or below 12 students.
- evidence that satisfies it: competitor fees, batch sizes and parent switching reasons from Satara (a verified listing, a visit or call by the founder, or parent responses). Evidence that the founder can differentiate on smaller batches and a known teacher without a price edge. Evidence that no national chain with a Satara centre sells kids' spoken English.
- why it matters: the generator itself found a ₹600 vs ₹1,000 gap but the price edge is easy to copy, and the supply of spoken-English sellers in Satara is in line with peer towns (not under-served).

### KE4
- dies unless: cash flow survives the calendar and the scaling limits. The calendar: SSC exams run 2027-02-15 to 2027-03-11, with Diwali and the summer holidays slow. The scaling limit: the 12 hours/week HARD cap must cover teaching, prep, parent communication, fee collection and marketing, and the Instagram skill is only basic (K4).
- threshold: (a) a 12-month cash model with enrolment dips of at least 30% in January-March for class 9-10 and in the holiday months does not push cumulative cash below -₹75,000 and returns to positive monthly cash by month 6 at conservative enrolment; (b) the weekly schedule needed for the enrolment used (batches, evenings, prep, admin) totals 12 hours or fewer; (c) monthly churn assumed is at least 10% in the conservative case.
- evidence that satisfies it: economics and simulation with seasonal and churn inputs from the founder's own teaching calendar (FK Q12), the SSC dates, and a written weekly hours schedule. The schedule should include the founder's current school duties if she still works there.
- why it matters: ₹600 per student means a thin margin. A ceiling of 3 batches of 25 gives ₹45,000/month gross, and one bad quarter or founder burnout ends the business. Class 10 attendance drops during exam preparation.

### KE5
- dies unless: the first 10 paying students can be reached through channels the founder controls or can access without a third party's permission, within 2 months and within the KE1 budget (conservative conversion, not 3% of 300).
- threshold: at conservative conversion (assume at most 2-3% of 300 reachable parents, about 6-9 sign-ups) the plan still shows at least 10 students by month 2. A shortfall must be covered by a named additional channel that costs less than ₹10,000 in total. The four WhatsApp groups (60-120 members each) must permit promotion, and no school rule or employment contract bars the founder from soliciting current or former students.
- evidence that satisfies it: group admin consent or group rules (founder-countable offline); the school's staff code of conduct or contract on private tuition; a trial class or paid pre-enrolment from at least a few parents before the room is rented; basic Instagram or a flyer test costing under ₹10,000.
- why it matters: the founder's reach is from WhatsApp groups and word of mouth that depend on admins, school rules and the parents' trust. Basic Instagram is weak, and directories show no local search habit for kids' spoken English.
