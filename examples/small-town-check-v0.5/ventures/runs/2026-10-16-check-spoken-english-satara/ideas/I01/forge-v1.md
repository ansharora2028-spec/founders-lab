## Core identity
- customer: Parents of class 6-10 students in Satara city who pay a monthly fee for an after-school spoken-English batch. Narrowed first segment: parents of class 8-10 students, reached through the founder's 4 parent WhatsApp groups and parents of students she taught.
- problem: Children study English as reading, writing and grammar and do not practise speaking; the nearest spoken-English class is reported to run batches of 30 (founder-reported, no review data).
- revenue model: Monthly fee per student, ₹600 as the user stated, paid by the parent by UPI or cash around salary date. No secondary revenue.
- pivot declared: no

## How it works
1. Finding it. A parent sees a message from the founder in one of her 4 parent WhatsApp groups (60-120 members each) or hears from a parent whose child she taught. The message gives the batch time, fee, batch cap and a free trial evening. [FOUNDER-COUNTED: FK Q5, Q11]
2. Joining. The parent replies on WhatsApp, the child attends one free trial hour, the parent pays the first month by UPI or cash.
3. What the child gets. One hour on weekday evenings, a small batch, mostly speaking: short talks, role-play, conversation prompts, correction of pronunciation and sentence building. Number of evenings per week, curriculum and batch cap are not stated by the user. [ASSUMPTION: 3 evenings per week, batch of 25 or fewer in total]
4. What the founder does each week (about 12 h, the K2 limit): about 3 teaching hours per batch, 1-2 hours of preparing prompts and printouts, 1 hour of WhatsApp replies, fee reminders and parent updates. Tools: a rented or borrowed room, whiteboard, phone for WhatsApp and UPI, a notebook for attendance and fees. Instagram is optional and secondary (K4, basic skill only).
5. Each month: fee reminders around the salary date, a short progress message per child to the parent.

Venue. The room near a school is a FLEXIBLE item (K9, ₹4,000-6,000 per month, [FOUNDER-ESTIMATE: FK Q7]). Whether a cheaper venue exists (founder's home, a hall rented by the hour) was not researched. The draft Act bars operating on recognised school premises, so a room in a school building is not an option if the Act applies (see Need gap).

## How it makes money
- who pays, for what, how much, how often: the parent of each enrolled child pays ₹600 per month (user's price) for a weekday-evening 1-hour spoken-English batch, collected once a month by UPI or cash.
- other revenue (if any): none identified. A free trial evening is a cost, not revenue.
- what one sale costs to deliver, and what drives the margin: the marginal cost per extra student is printed material and founder time (the founder's hours are fixed at 12 per week, K2). The main cost is the fixed venue (₹4,000-6,000 per month if rented), so margin is set by head count per batch against that fixed cost. If the draft Act applies in the form published, the head count above 25 triggers registration, which caps the unregistered business at about 25 students; this is a ceiling on revenue, not a cost to deliver.
- Price room. Local reference prices are ₹800-1,500 (tuition, counted), about ₹1,000 (spoken-English class, heard) and ₹2,000 (seller listing); ₹600 sits below all of them. A higher price for a capped batch is a modelling option for the economics modeler; it is not tested here. [ASSUMPTION: parents do not read a lower price as lower quality; unverified]
