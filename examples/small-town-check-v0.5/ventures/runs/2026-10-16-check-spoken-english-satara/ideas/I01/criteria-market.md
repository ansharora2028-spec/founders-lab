## Criteria

### KM1
- dies unless: at least 2 named operators of in-person spoken-English (or English-speaking) classes for school-age children (any age span inside class 5-10) have visible paying customers in Satara city or in peer towns of Maharashtra with population 50,000 to 3,00,000, at a published or reported fee of ₹500/month or more.
- threshold: >=2 operators, each with a published fee >=₹500/month AND at least one sign of real customers (>=10 dated public reviews, or a stated enrolment of >=20 students, or dated batch/admission posts within the last 12 months). Directory listings with no reviews or orders do not count.
- evidence that satisfies it: Justdial or Google Maps pages with review counts, operator websites or social pages with fee and batch posts, fee notices, local news features. Desk research is enough; an offline count by the founder (names, batch sizes, fees seen on notices) counts as FOUNDER-COUNTED only and gives at most INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE.
- why it matters: demand evidence. Today the only kids' anchors in Satara are founder hearsay (about ₹1,000/month, batches of 30) and one seller listing at ₹2,000 with no customers shown.

### KM2
- dies unless: the Satara city catchment holds enough class 6-10 students that the break-even number of paying students is a small share of them.
- threshold: >=6,000 students enrolled in classes 6-10 in schools in Satara city (all managements), so that 30 paying students is at most 0.5% of them. If only the 6 schools within 3 km can be counted, >=2,500 students in classes 6-10 in those schools.
- evidence that satisfies it: UDISE+ school-wise enrolment, Satara Municipal Council or Zilla Parishad education office data, school-published strength, or the founder's counted enrolment of the 6 nearby schools.
- why it matters: market size in the target market. The only size figure now is an unverified assumption of 9,000-12,000 children aged 11-15 derived from population.

### KM3
- dies unless: the addressable age group stays large enough under the Maharashtra private-coaching rules that are in force or most likely to be in force when batches start.
- threshold: either (a) the enacted Act, or the draft text, excludes language or spoken-English classes, or drops or lowers the under-13 bar, or (b) if the under-13 bar applies, class 8-10 enrolment in the Satara catchment is still >=3,600 (the 6,000 of KM2 less roughly 40% for class 6-7), and batches of 25 or fewer remain outside registration.
- evidence that satisfies it: official text of the Maharashtra Private Coaching Centers (Registration and Regulation) Act, 2026 (draft or enacted) from the state gazette or School Education Department, with the definition of "coaching" and the age rule; enrolment from the KM2 sources.
- why it matters: timing and market access. The draft bars under-13s, and class 6-7 pupils are mostly 11-13, so a third to two fifths of the stated customer group may be illegal to enrol.

### KM4
- dies unless: in-person kids' English-speaking or skills classes in small towns have survived long enough to show repeat paying demand, and the graveyard does not show them closing for lack of paying parents.
- threshold: >=2 in-person kids' spoken-English or English-skills operators in Satara city or peer towns (Maharashtra, population 50,000 to 3,00,000) operating >=24 months with dated evidence, AND no more than 1 documented closure in those towns attributed to weak enrolment or non-payment.
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pages or reviews with dated posts across 2+ years, Wayback Machine snapshots, Justdial or Maps review history, local news or founder-counted names of closed classes with reason.
- why it matters: graveyard and timing. No Satara launch or shutdown of a spoken-English business was found, so survival and failure rates are unknown.

### KM5
- dies unless: official data shows parents of secondary-level students in urban Maharashtra already spend on private coaching at a level that makes ₹600/month (₹7,200/year) plausible as an add-on.
- threshold: Maharashtra urban share of students at upper-primary/secondary level taking private coaching >=25%, AND a reported average annual coaching spend per coaching student at that level >=₹6,000 (or per-month equivalent >=₹500).
- evidence that satisfies it: NSS 80th round Comprehensive Modular Survey: Education (2025) tables for Maharashtra (urban, by level), from MoSPI or an official report; not secondary news summaries of national averages.
- why it matters: existing spending on a substitute. The national figures cited so far (about 27% take coaching; average annual spend ₹3,988 urban across all levels) sit below ₹7,200/year, so the price may be above the typical add-on spend.
