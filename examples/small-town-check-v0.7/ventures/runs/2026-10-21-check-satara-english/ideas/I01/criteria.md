## Market criteria
### KM1
- dies unless: at least 3 spoken-English providers in Satara city or Karad have verifiable paying customers, and at least 2 of them show a published or confirmed price of ₹500 per month or more for school-age students.
- threshold: 3 providers with a verifiable paid-service signal (a dated price list, enrolment or batch counts, or at least 5 customer reviews each), and at least 2 verified prices at ₹500 per month or more. Today only 1 Satara price is visible (Success, ₹2,000 per month), so the criterion is not yet met.
- evidence that satisfies it: a VERIFIED source for each provider (provider page, directory listing with price, social page showing fees, reviews). A price confirmed by a documented call or visit by the founder counts as FOUNDER-COUNTED only and cannot make this criterion MET.
- why it matters: rule 3 requires verified demand evidence, and the current Satara evidence is 4 directory stubs with 1 price.

### KM2
- dies unless: the target market is large enough that the contract-fitting enrolment of 30 to 40 students is a small share of it.
- threshold: at least 8,000 students in classes 6 to 10 in Satara city schools (so 40 students is at most 0.5%), and at least 1,500 of them in English-medium or semi-English schools.
- evidence that satisfies it: UDISE+ or Maharashtra education department enrolment by class for Satara city, or a sum of school-level UDISE+ figures. A population-ratio estimate counts only as ASSUMPTION.
- why it matters: Satara has about 1.2 lakh people (2011), and the demand base for one age band in one town has not been measured.

### KM3
- dies unless: there is evidence of an unmet gap that a ₹600 small-batch class fills, and not only a lower price.
- threshold: at least one of the following. (a) Two or more Satara providers' batch sizes are verified at 20 or more students. (b) At least 10 Satara parents of class 6 to 10 students, recorded by name or initials and date, state that they would pay ₹600 per month for a batch of 25 or fewer with a stated speaking-time promise. (c) At least 5 public Satara reviews or posts complaining about lack of speaking practice.
- evidence that satisfies it: for (a) and (c), a source URL. For (b), a dated founder-run parent survey. (b) is FOUNDER-COUNTED and gives INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE at best, not MET. If only (b) is missing, report it as an offline-only gap.
- why it matters: the gap currently rests on "founder heard" claims (01-local-market C44, C45), and the peer-town benchmark shows no under-supply.

### KM4
- dies unless: at least one offline spoken-English or English-coaching operator in a Satara-like town (population under 2 lakh) has operated for 2 years or more serving school-age students, and no cheaper, better-positioned substitute already takes the segment.
- threshold: at least 1 survival case of 2 or more years (a current Satara-city case such as Success at 8 years counts if its school-age focus is verified), and no verified Satara provider selling class 6 to 10 group batches at ₹700 or less per month with batches of 15 or fewer.
- evidence that satisfies it: a dated provider page or directory profile showing years in business and target students, plus a check of Satara providers' fees and batch sizes. For the graveyard, name the closed operators found, the dates and the stated causes, or state that none were found after searching.
- why it matters: the BYJU'S closures show offline tuition can fail on rent and teacher cost, and a direct ₹600 undercut would wipe out the price advantage.

### KM5
- dies unless: the ₹600 price is defensible against the cheap and free substitutes parents have.
- threshold: the plan names a measurable difference from AI practice at ₹0 to ₹399 per month (ChatGPT Go ₹399, SpeakX ₹299) and from school-subject tuition at ₹800 to ₹1,500 per subject. The difference is at least 10 minutes of individual speaking time per student per hour, and a batch of 25 or fewer. Both must be deliverable in a 1-hour class, which needs a batch of about 6 or fewer, or a pair or group-rotation format that the plan describes.
- evidence that satisfies it: the plan's class format with a minutes-per-student calculation, plus a verified price list of substitutes (the 02-signals sources count). Competitor or vendor marketing blogs do not count as proof of parent preference.
- why it matters: the speaking-time promise is the only stated differentiator, and a 1-hour class of 20 students gives each student about 3 minutes of speaking unless the format changes.

## Execution criteria
### KE1
- dies unless: a working first version can be delivered and run within the HARD contract limits for capital, hours and loss.
- threshold: start-up cost of at most ₹40,000 (deposit, fittings, printing, signage), and start-up plus 3 months of rent at the high estimate (₹6,000) plus supplies at most ₹75,000 and not above ₹63,000. Weekly hours at the planned batch count (teaching, preparation, admin, marketing, fee collection) of at most 12. No hired help is assumed in the base case, and hired help counts against the capital.
- evidence that satisfies it: an itemised cost list with a rent quote for a room near a named school (at least 2 listings or quotes) and an hours table by activity. A room quote is FOUNDER-COUNTED, and published rent listings are VERIFIED.
- why it matters: K1 (₹75,000), K2 (12 hours per week) and K5 (cannot lose more than ₹75,000) are all HARD. The generator's own hours estimate leaves zero slack at 12.

### KE2
- dies unless: the first paying students arrive within the contract's 2-month window, with a realistic path from the founder's reach to paid enrolments.
- threshold: first ₹ received by 2026-12-21 (60 days from the 2026-10-21 contract date), and at least 10 paying students by day 60. This needs a conversion of at most 3.3% of the 300 parents reachable in month 1 (Q11), or an equal count from named pre-enrolments.
- evidence that satisfies it: a dated plan from room confirmation to first class, with no permit or registration blocker that takes more than 30 days. Peer-town or desk evidence on typical conversion from parent WhatsApp groups to paid enrolment counts as PEER-TOWN. The Q11 reach of 300 parents is FOUNDER-COUNTED and cannot make this criterion MET.
- why it matters: K6 (2 months to first revenue) is HARD. Diwali and holiday slowness, and the February to March exam season (SSC 15 Feb to 11 Mar 2027), compress the usable window.

### KE3
- dies unless: unit economics stay positive in the conservative case and give the founder a meaningful return for 12 hours a week.
- threshold: break-even at 12 students or fewer at ₹600 with rent at ₹6,000, supplies at ₹500 and any registration or GST cost included. In the conservative case (15 students on average over the first 6 months, 20% monthly churn, no enrolment in the 4 weeks around Diwali and in May) the 6-month cumulative cash result is above ₹0 after start-up cost, and at 30 students the founder's net is at least ₹10,000 per month.
- evidence that satisfies it: a worked model using verified or clearly labelled inputs: rent (listing or quote), price (the contract), churn (labelled ASSUMPTION or sourced), seasonality (sourced exam and holiday dates). Per rule 3, the conservative case must pass, and a base case alone is not enough.
- why it matters: the generator's own math gives ₹3,000 to ₹5,000 per month at 15 students, below the founder's likely opportunity cost, with the room rent taking 45% to 65% of revenue.

### KE4
- dies unless: the plan can operate lawfully at its proposed scale under current law and the draft Maharashtra coaching Act (as drafted and in a plausible softened form), at a cost inside the contract.
- threshold: (a) the status of the draft Act as of 2026-10-21 is checked and stated. (b) A compliant operating model exists for class 6 students (usually aged 11 to 12) if the under-13 bar survives, with at least 80% of the target class 6 to 10 band still servable, or a parental-consent route is confirmed. (c) Batches stay at 25 students or fewer per centre, or registration is budgeted at no more than ₹10,000 and 30 days. (d) Shops and Establishments registration, GST threshold and DPDP parental-consent duties are each checked and costed. (e) The founder's own employment status is confirmed not to bar them (the draft bars hiring teachers of recognised schools).
- evidence that satisfies it: the draft or final text of the Act, the Maharashtra Labour Department rule page, current CGST turnover rules and the DPDP Rules, each cited with a URL. Where the Act is still draft, a MET needs a compliant plan under the strict reading. A sourced "no final version" is acceptable.
- why it matters: the draft carries fines of ₹1 lakh to ₹50 lakh against a ₹75,000 capital and a low risk appetite (K5), and class 6 sits at the age bar.

### KE5
- dies unless: customer acquisition does not depend on a single channel the founder cannot control, and the idea survives the likely incumbent response.
- threshold: the plan names at least 2 acquisition channels (for example, parent WhatsApp groups plus school-teacher referrals or Instagram), with the WhatsApp-group channel supported by evidence that group admins permit promotion of a paid class in at least 3 of the 4 groups. A written response plan covers an incumbent price cut or a copy: Success and the bus-stand class are each able to match ₹600 or open a small-batch class, and PhysicsWallah's Foundation batches for classes 8 to 10. The plan shows that the model stays at break-even (KE3) if enrolment is 40% below plan.
- evidence that satisfies it: the founder's confirmation from the group admins (FOUNDER-COUNTED), an acquisition plan with each channel's expected enrolments, and a sensitivity run on the KE3 model at 40% fewer students. Incumbent behaviour in Satara is thin online, so a founder-visited price check counts as FOUNDER-COUNTED. If it is the only missing proof, it is an offline-only gap.
- why it matters: all current distribution evidence is the founder's own 4 WhatsApp groups (FOUNDER-COUNTED) and word of mouth, and the founder's marketing skill is FLEXIBLE with no stated unlock condition.
