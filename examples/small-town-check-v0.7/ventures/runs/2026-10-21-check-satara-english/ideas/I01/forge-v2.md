## Core identity
- customer: parents of class 8-10 students (age about 13-16) in Satara city, who pay; class 6-7 students are a second, optional batch only if the coaching law (see C26) allows it. Narrowing from class 6-10 to class 8-10 stays within the same customer type (parents of school students, B2C).
- problem: the child gets homework-and-grammar tuition but little spoken-English practice; the existing spoken-English options are either priced at about ₹1,000-2,000 a month or run large batches (founder heard).
- revenue model: flat monthly fee per student, paid by the parent by UPI or cash around salary date. Round 2 changes the offer price from ₹600 to ₹800 as the base case (₹600 kept as a test cell). This is an offer change inside the same revenue model (same payer, same per-student monthly fee), not a pivot. The ₹800 value is not backed by a sourced school-age spoken-English price (see Fixes, KE3/FE2).
- pivot declared: no
- STRETCH USED: none. K4 (basic Instagram marketing) is used as stated; no stretch range exists.

## How it works
1. Finding it. The founder posts a short message in her 4 parent WhatsApp groups (60-120 members each) and messages parents of past students: "free demo class, Saturday, 30 minutes, class 8-10". Parents reply on WhatsApp. A WhatsApp Business number and an Instagram page with 2-3 short student speaking clips (with parental consent) back it up.
2. Trying it. Parent and child attend a free 30-minute demo in the rented room. The founder runs a short level check (read aloud, 2-minute talk on a topic).
3. Joining. Parent pays the first month, ₹800 (₹600 in the test cell), by UPI (QR at the room) or cash and gets a written receipt (also what the draft law asks for, C26).
4. What the child receives. A 1-hour weekday-evening session in a batch capped at 20 (below the draft law's 25-student line). Each session: about 35 minutes of student talk in pairs and small groups (role-play, picture description, daily-situation dialogues, short talks), 15 minutes of correction, 10 minutes of vocabulary. The founder teaches in English with Marathi explanation when needed. Parents get a one-line WhatsApp progress note every two weeks.
   - Speaking-time calculation (round 2, for a batch of 20, 60-minute session): 35 minutes of pair talk, 10 pairs, partners alternate turns. At equal turn-taking, each student speaks about 17 minutes; at 60% effective use (noise, Marathi drift, setup), each student speaks about 10.5 minutes of English per hour. Teacher listening: 35 minutes divided over 10 pairs gives about 3.5 minutes of direct teacher listening per pair per session, plus 15 minutes of whole-class correction (about 45 seconds per student if every student is corrected once). Rotation: pairs change every 7 minutes (5 rounds), so each student talks with 5 partners per session. [ASSUMPTION: the 60% effective-use factor and equal turn-taking are design assumptions with no observed data; the founder's recorded demo class can measure actual speaking time.] The 10-minute floor is met only at 60% use or better, with no margin below that.
5. Weekly founder work (12 hours, K2): about 10 hours teaching (2 batches, 5 weekday evenings, or 3 batches x 3 days; to be chosen), about 2 hours on lesson prep, fee collection, WhatsApp replies and one Instagram post. Tools: rented room with whiteboard, WhatsApp Business, UPI QR, a Google Sheet for attendance and fees.
6. Renewal. Fees are due around salary date; a reminder goes out on WhatsApp on the 1st-5th.

## How it makes money
- who pays, for what, how much, how often: the parent pays per enrolled student, monthly, for one weekday-evening hour of spoken-English practice; ₹800 per month base case (offer change declared in Core identity), ₹600 in a split test cell. The only visible Satara competitor price is ₹2,000, and the heard bus-stand price is about ₹1,000.
- other revenue (if any): optional short holiday intensive (Diwali, summer) paid as a one-off batch fee; not assumed in the base case. Not a new revenue model (same customer, same per-student fee).
- what one sale costs to deliver, and what drives the margin: delivery cost per extra student is close to zero up to the batch cap; fixed cost is room rent and small supplies. Margin is driven by students per batch, number of batches the 12 hours allow, and the price. Acquisition is mostly founder time in WhatsApp groups. Room rent: the founder's estimate is ₹4,000-6,000 per month (heard); the only Satara commercial rents seen in round 2 are far higher (see C29), so the rent input is uncertain upward.
