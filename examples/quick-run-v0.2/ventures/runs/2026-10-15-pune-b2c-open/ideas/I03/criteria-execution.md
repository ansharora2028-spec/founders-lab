## Criteria

### KE1
- dies unless: a working first version (rental agreement template, move-in condition report format, deposit-return letter in English, Hindi and Marathi, UPI collection, WhatsApp/Instagram delivery) can be built and sold with at most 10 hours per week (K2, HARD) and at most ₹60,000 of the ₹1,00,000 capital (K1, HARD), including any paid lawyer review and freelancer translation, using only the skills in K3 or hires within K4.
- threshold: build cost (lawyer review + translation + tools + ads) <= ₹60,000; build plus sales effort <= 10 hours in any week; no stretch hours used (K2 has no stretch range).
- evidence that satisfies it: itemised cost list with sourced quotes (advocate fee for reviewing a tenancy template and demand letter in Pune, freelancer rate for Hindi/Marathi legal translation, tool subscriptions) and an hour-by-hour build and weekly operating plan; the plan must show how the per-order work (personalising a deposit-return letter, reviewing a customer's photo report) stays within 10 hours at the volume assumed.
- why it matters: tests the contract's capital and time limits directly; the K1 loss limit is also the K5 "cannot lose" line.

### KE2
- dies unless: first paid sale happens within 3 months (K6, HARD) of start, and the customer-acquisition path that produces it is open to a non-resident of the target communities, meaning PG, college and IT-park WhatsApp groups and Instagram let a third-party seller post or advertise, with a stated number of paid orders reachable in that window.
- threshold: at least 1 paid order by day 90; the plan names the specific channels, their posting rules (admin approval, ad policy for legal or rental services) and the expected number of orders to cover the capital spent by day 90.
- evidence that satisfies it: platform policy pages (Meta ad policy, WhatsApp group rules, Instagram), evidence that Pune PG and IT-park communities accept paid promotion, and a comparable seller (any city) selling tenant or rental documents to consumers via these channels with visible orders or reviews.
- why it matters: Local-market scout found Instagram, paid ads and offline college/IT-park networks "not researched" (01-local-market.md, distribution table), so the only stated channel is unverified; a ₹399 item cannot carry paid acquisition costs.

### KE3
- dies unless: the product can be sold lawfully by a non-lawyer in Maharashtra. Specifically, the demand letter and the personalised agreement drafting do not count as unauthorised practice of law under the Advocates Act, 1961, or are delivered under an advocate's engagement or as clearly generic templates, with the arrangement costed inside KE1.
- threshold: a written basis (statute, Supreme Court or Bar Council position, or a named advocate's opinion) that the exact deliverables are permitted; a deposit-return demand letter personalised to a customer's facts must be shown to be either permitted or routed through an advocate.
- evidence that satisfies it: the Advocates Act provisions and case law on drafting as "practice of law", plus a working incumbent in India that sells personalised legal letters or agreements to consumers without being an advocate firm and has not been stopped, or an advocate willing to sign on at a fixed fee.
- why it matters: Supreme Court reasoning reported in search results treats drafting of instruments as practice of law and applies the regulatory mechanism to non-litigation work too [source: https://bcajonline.org/journal/practice-of-law-foreign-law-firms-are-not-eligible-to-open-liaison-offices-or-to-practice-law-in-india-even-giving-an-opinion-on-a-legal-matter-amounts-to-practise-of-law-advocates-act/]. Whether a template or letter service falls inside it was not answered in the search; a legal exposure with no cap conflicts with K5 low risk.

### KE4
- dies unless: the rental agreement in the kit is valid and usable in Maharashtra, so a tenant who signs it holds a document that is not worthless as evidence. The kit must handle stamp duty and registration of leave and licence agreements (or clearly state that the landlord must do it and show tenants will still pay for a kit that depends on landlord action).
- threshold: the kit's agreement follows the Maharashtra leave-and-licence format; the registration and stamp-duty route (including the state online process) is documented; the product states who must register and what happens if the landlord does not.
- evidence that satisfies it: statute text of Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999 and the Maharashtra stamp duty schedule, plus the Maharashtra IGR online registration process, showing the tenant-side value of the kit survives these rules. Secondary sources say registration is required with the duty on the landlord, that unregistered agreements are weak as evidence, and that the state has online drafting, stamping and registration since 2025-05-01; none was checked against the statute [source: https://housing.com/news/stamp-duty-registration-laws-rentals-maharashtra/]. Another source reports the 2025 online system and the end of intermediaries [source: https://rentu.in/news/10001371/maharashtra-pushes-online-registration-of-leave-and-licence-agreements].
- why it matters: if a state portal already offers drafting, stamping and registration, a ₹399 free-template-based kit sells something that cannot be enforced alone and that the state process supplies; and a defective document creates liability for the seller.

### KE5
- dies unless: the kit survives incumbents and free substitutes in Pune, meaning existing rental-agreement providers (legal-tech platforms, online stamp-paper and agreement sellers, property portals, registration agents) and free templates do not already supply the same deliverables at or below ₹399, and a tenant-protection bundle (condition report plus deposit letter) has at least 3 existing paying customers somewhere in India.
- threshold: at most 2 incumbents selling the full bundle at or below ₹399 in Pune; at least 3 verifiable paid examples (reviews, orders, a listed price) of the bundle or its parts sold to tenants; the idea's own note that free templates are widely available is addressed with a named paid differentiator.
- evidence that satisfies it: a competitor price table with live URLs (rental agreement sellers, tenant-side apps, move-in inspection apps), and evidence of copy risk, meaning how long a copycat needs (assumed days, since the product is documents) and what protects the kit (local-language letters, community trust, brand).
- why it matters: a document pack has no technical moat, and free alternatives set the price anchor; a copy at lower price on first success ends the margin.

### KE6
- dies unless: a tenant's willingness to buy is reachable inside the contract's data and platform limits, meaning the kit can be sold without collecting personal data that triggers cost, and without relying on a platform that can ban the category. It must collect and store tenant ID, photos of premises and landlord details under a stated data-handling plan (storage, consent, deletion) that costs under ₹5,000 and fits DPDP duties that begin around 2027-05-13, and must not depend on a single platform (Instagram ads, WhatsApp Business API) whose policy change would stop all sales.
- threshold: data-handling plan with cost <= ₹5,000 and under 2 hours per week; at least 2 independent acquisition channels each able to produce orders; no single platform carrying more than 70 percent of planned orders.
- evidence that satisfies it: DPDP Rules text for obligations on a small seller holding photos and ID (national) [source: https://www.mondaq.com/india/data-protection/1708164/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-notified], Meta/WhatsApp commerce and business policies for legal or housing offerings, and a channel split with order estimates.
- why it matters: the product handles tenant and landlord personal data and a dispute document; a platform ban or data complaint is a risk this founder cannot absorb at ₹1,00,000 with a full-time job (K9).
