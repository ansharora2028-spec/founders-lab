## Raw idea
### I01
- pitch: Weekend spoken-English conversation clubs for Pune college students, held in cafés, ₹499 per month
- customer: Pune college student, paying ₹499 per month from their own or family money (not stated by the user: who pays, student or parent; ASSUMPTION: the student pays by UPI, since the user says "paid by the college student")
- problem: College students want to practise speaking English with other people, and the in-person options cost far more than ₹499 or are not built for casual practice. (Problem as the user implies it; not stated in detail by the user. ASSUMPTION: the pain is confidence and fluency in speaking.)
- revenue model: Monthly membership of ₹499 per student, for access to the weekend café sessions
- how it works: A student joins the club for the month and pays ₹499 by UPI. On weekends they go to a partner café in Pune and join a group conversation in English with other students. (not stated by the user: how long the session is, how many sessions per month, or who leads it; ASSUMPTION: about 90 minutes, 2 to 4 weekend sessions a month, led by the founder or a hired freelancer, with topics and games prepared beforehand.) The founder finds students through social media and WhatsApp groups, books the café, collects payments and runs the sessions, using about 10 hours a week.
- need gap: Today a Pune student who wants to practise has these choices. Paid in-person institutes cost roughly ₹4,000-15,000 per month (L01; promotional sources, unverified). Tutors cost about ₹702 per hour (C04 in 01). AI apps cost ₹150-₹299 per month, for example SpeakX at ₹299 (S02, L02). Free options are open Toastmasters clubs (dues not found), free language-exchange matching and free WhatsApp or Telegram groups (L02, S06). The gap the idea aims at is a human, in-person, casual option priced between the free options and the institutes. Comparison blogs say apps lack real human practice, but they are vendor-written (S08, weak). No paid Pune café English club was found (L03, S06), but that could mean no demand as easily as an open gap. Whether students will pay ₹499 is a guess; no Pune student spending data exists (L05). Whether cafés will host a group on these terms is also not known (L04 shows cafés host community events).
- how it makes money: Students pay ₹499 each month, collected by UPI (L06), which costs little to collect. Cost per student per month is not known. ASSUMPTION: about ₹50-150 per student for materials, payment fees and any venue fee, if cafés host in return for the students' food and drink orders; this is untested. ASSUMPTION: if the founder's monthly costs (marketing, venue fees, a freelancer for some sessions) are about ₹15,000-20,000, then about 35-50 paying students a month cover them; with 15 students per session, that is roughly 3 groups. Earning anything for the founder's own time would need more. The ₹1,00,000 capital limit and the 3-month limit to first revenue are the user's constraints; this idea could take a first payment within weeks (ASSUMPTION), but retention after the first month is unknown. The economics modeler does the real model.

## Market criteria
### KM1
- dies unless: there is demand evidence that paid, recurring, in-person group English-conversation practice has paying customers at a low monthly price in India.
- threshold: at least 2 operators in Pune, or at least 3 operators in other Indian cities, each charging at or below ₹2,000 per month (or per-session pricing that works out to that), each with an observable sign of paying customers (for example 20 or more public reviews, published attendance or member counts, or repeated ticketed events over at least 3 months).
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pages, Meetup, Luma, Instagram or Google Maps listings with prices and dated attendance or reviews; paid-event ticket pages; press coverage with member numbers. Vendor claims without a dated, checkable sign of customers do not count.
- why it matters: the local scan found no paid café English club in Pune (01-local-market.md#C19), so existence of paying customers for this format is unproven.

### KM2
- dies unless: there is evidence that college students in Pune (or, failing that, students in another Indian metro, labelled as such) pay out of their own or family discretionary money for recurring non-academic self-improvement or hobby products at around ₹499 per month.
- threshold: at least 2 independent examples of recurring student-paid products priced between ₹300 and ₹1,000 per month with a verifiable paying-user count of 1,000 or more or a sourced student-spend figure showing monthly discretionary spend of at least ₹2,000 per student.
- evidence that satisfies it: company filings or press with paying-subscriber counts (for example app subscriptions aimed at students), student-spend surveys from a named research firm with sample size and city, or operator pricing pages with enrolment figures.
- why it matters: no Pune student spend data exists (01-local-market.md#C14), so affordability and willingness to pay at ₹499 is untested.

### KM3
- dies unless: the forge shows a paid human-led English-practice product surviving next to free and cheaper substitutes, so ₹499 is not undercut by them.
- threshold: at least 1 documented operator (any Indian city) of paid live-human English speaking practice, priced at or below ₹1,500 per month, that has operated for 12 months or more while free groups (Toastmasters, WhatsApp or Telegram groups, language exchange) and ₹299-per-month apps such as SpeakX are available; plus the Pune Toastmasters dues for the open clubs stated with a source, so the price gap to ₹499 is known.
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pages with founding dates and current pricing, archived pages or dated reviews showing 12 or more months of operation, Toastmasters club or district fee pages.
- why it matters: SpeakX sells at ₹299 per month with a claimed 200,000 paying subscribers (02-signals.md#S01, S02) and free options exist (01-local-market.md#C15, C17), so the idea sells against cheaper and free alternatives.

### KM4
- dies unless: the graveyard check does not show a pattern of similar ventures failing for demand reasons.
- threshold: a documented search of at least 5 distinct queries (English, plus Hindi or Marathi terms) for shut-down or abandoned offline English-conversation clubs, paid language meetups or student paid-community startups in India; and, among any found, fewer than 2 whose stated or evidenced cause of death is low willingness to pay or poor retention. If 2 or more such failures are found, the idea dies unless the forge gives dated evidence of what is different.
- evidence that satisfies it: news, founder post-mortems, archived pages, Meetup group history (member counts and last event dates), and the search log itself.
- why it matters: the local scan found no failure data, which it states is absence of evidence only (01-local-market.md#C09).

### KM5
- dies unless: the reachable Pune student market is large enough that about 50 paying members is a small share of those with the need.
- threshold: a sourced count of college and university students in Pune (district or city) of at least 150,000, and a sourced indicator that at least 15% of Indian college students report low spoken-English confidence or a wish to improve it (national data is acceptable only if labelled national). Under these figures 50 payers would be under 0.5% of the students with the need in Pune.
- evidence that satisfies it: AISHE or Savitribai Phule Pune University enrolment tables for Pune; employability or English-proficiency surveys with named sample and year (for example ones published by an assessment firm).
- why it matters: no Pune student count was found (01-local-market.md#C28), and the retention of a small paid club depends on a large enough pool.

## Execution criteria
### KE1
- dies unless: a plan to run enough weekend groups to cover costs (the ~35-50 paying students the raw idea implies, i.e. about 3 groups) fits inside the user's HARD limits of 10 hours/week (K2, outside the full-time job, K9) and ₹1,00,000 total spend (K1, K5), with the first rupee collected within 3 months (K6).
- threshold: total founder hours at the steady-state size (sales and WhatsApp, café booking, payments and attendance admin, preparing topics, plus every session the founder personally leads, including travel) of 10 hours/week or less; cumulative spend to first month-3 of ₹60,000 or less (leaving at least ₹40,000 reserve); first paid member within 90 days of 2026-10-15. Hours that depend on a hired facilitator count only if the facilitator's cost is inside the ₹1,00,000.
- evidence that satisfies it: a week-by-week hours table with session length and count taken from named comparable Pune operators or Toastmasters-style meeting formats; a cost table with sourced Pune freelancer/facilitator rates and café terms; no stretch of K2 (it is HARD).
- why it matters: weekend sessions are a fixed block of hours that grow with each added group, so the 10-hour cap may bind before the break-even student count is reached.

### KE2
- dies unless: at least 3 Pune cafés (or an equivalent venue type) are shown to host a recurring paid-group weekend session of about 90 minutes, 12-15 people, on terms the founder can afford, and without a platform or venue that can end the arrangement at will.
- threshold: venue cost per session of ₹1,000 or less in cash (or a minimum-spend model where members' own orders cover it, with that minimum-spend evidenced at or below ₹150 per head); at least 3 distinct venues; the arrangement must not need a written exclusivity or a revenue share above 20% of the ₹499 fee.
- evidence that satisfies it: a published café event/private-booking rate card, or listings of Pune cafés that host paid workshops/meetups with their fee or minimum spend, or a documented quote from cafés. An event listing alone, such as a free Luma meetup, does not show a rate for a paid group.
- why it matters: the café is the delivery channel and the only cost line the generator could not size; a café can raise its fee, drop weekend tables at peak hours, or object to a group occupying seats without sufficient orders.

### KE3
- dies unless: the legal and registration cost and effort of running paid, recurring group sessions for students in Pune, including those under 18, is shown to be both permissible and affordable within the contract.
- threshold: total registration, licence, insurance and compliance cost in year 1 of ₹10,000 or less; no licence or accreditation that takes longer than 60 days or that the user does not hold (K8 bars anything needing a licence they lack); a determination, from a primary source or professional, on (a) Maharashtra Shops and Establishments registration / PMC Gumasta for a café-hosted, home-run service, (b) GST registration trigger for services (the ₹20 lakh threshold is not expected to bind, but inter-state or platform supply can trigger it), (c) whether charging or hosting minors needs consent or safeguarding steps, and (d) liability if a student is hurt or harassed at a session.
- evidence that satisfies it: the Maharashtra Labour Department or PMC pages or a registered professional's written opinion; the GST portal rules; an insurance quote for event or public-liability cover in Pune.
- why it matters: the market scout found no primary-source answer (01 C21-C22), the user has low risk appetite (K5), and a safeguarding or liability incident with young adults in public venues is an unbudgeted loss.

### KE4
- dies unless: the likely response of incumbents and free substitutes does not remove the price position within the first 6 months of operation.
- threshold: evidence that none of these already offers, or can add at a marginal cost close to zero, an in-person or live group English-speaking session in Pune at ₹1,000/month or less: (a) Pune coaching institutes with weekend/demo batches, (b) open Toastmasters clubs (free to attend or low dues), (c) college English/communication societies and free WhatsApp/Telegram or Meetup groups, (d) SpeakX at ₹299/month or similar apps that could add live human sessions. At least the dues of Toastmasters clubs and the prices of at least 3 Pune institutes' shortest or weekend plans must be found.
- evidence that satisfies it: Toastmasters club dues from toastmasters.org or club pages; price pages or demo-batch pages of at least 3 Pune institutes; app feature pages showing whether live human or group sessions are offered in India; a count of free English clubs at the largest Pune colleges.
- why it matters: the scout found free and cheaper substitutes already present (01 L02, 02 S02), and ₹499 has no moat if an institute runs one cheap weekend batch or a college club does the same free.

### KE5
- dies unless: the delivery model keeps a positive contribution margin when the founder is not running every session, and it cannot be copied at no cost by a member or hired facilitator.
- threshold: facilitator cost at a sourced Pune market rate (not the founder's unpaid time) plus venue plus payment fees of 50% or less of ₹499 per student per month, at 10 paying students per group and 4 sessions per month; a retention assumption of no more than 50% of members renewing for month 2 must still leave the monthly cash margin positive after the ₹1,00,000 is partly spent on acquisition; a named barrier (brand, content, location, vetted facilitators) that stops a facilitator or the top members from starting the same group on WhatsApp and charging directly.
- evidence that satisfies it: sourced Pune freelancer / English tutor hourly rates (the scout has ₹702/hour for tutors, 01 C04); payment-gateway fee schedule (UPI/Razorpay or similar); retention figures from comparable subscription clubs or offline classes; evidence of how comparable community businesses handled facilitator or member poaching.
- why it matters: at ₹499 and 10 to 15 students a group, one paid hour of a facilitator at ₹702 consumes more than the per-session revenue share, so the margin depends on who leads and on repeat attendance, and success invites cheap copies.
