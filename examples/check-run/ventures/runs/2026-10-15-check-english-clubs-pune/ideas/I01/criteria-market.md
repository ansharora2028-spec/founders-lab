## Criteria
### KM1
- dies unless: there is demand evidence that paid, recurring, in-person group English-conversation practice has paying customers at a low monthly price in India.
- threshold: at least 2 operators in Pune, or at least 3 operators in other Indian cities, each charging at or below ₹2,000 per month (or per-session pricing that works out to that), each with an observable sign of paying customers (for example 20 or more public reviews, published attendance or member counts, or repeated ticketed events over at least 3 months).
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pages, Meetup, Luma, Instagram or Google Maps listings with prices and dated attendance or reviews; paid-event ticket pages; press coverage with member numbers. Vendor claims without a dated, checkable sign of customers do not count.
- why it matters: the local scan found no paid café English club in Pune (01-local-market.md#C19), so existence of paying customers for this format is unproven.

### KM2
- dies unless: there is evidence that college students in Pune (or, failing that, students in another Indian metro, labelled as such) pay out of their own or family discretionary money for recurring non-academic self-improvement or hobby products at around ₹499 per month.
- threshold: at least 2 independent examples of recurring student-paid products priced between ₹300 and ₹1,000 per month with a verifiable paying-user count of 1,000 or more or a sourced student-spend figure showing monthly discretionary spend of at least ₹2,000 per student.
- evidence that satisfies it: company filings or press with paying-subscriber counts (for example app subscriptions aimed at students), student-spend surveys from a named research firm with sample size and city, or operator pricing pages with enrolment figures.
- why it matters: no Pune student spend data exists (01-local-market.md#C14), so affordability and willingness to pay at ₹499 is untested.

### KM3
- dies unless: the forge shows a paid human-led English-practice product surviving next to free and cheaper substitutes, so ₹499 is not undercut by them.
- threshold: at least 1 documented operator (any Indian city) of paid live-human English speaking practice, priced at or below ₹1,500 per month, that has operated for 12 months or more while free groups (Toastmasters, WhatsApp or Telegram groups, language exchange) and ₹299-per-month apps such as SpeakX are available; plus the Pune Toastmasters dues for the open clubs stated with a source, so the price gap to ₹499 is known.
- evidence that satisfies it: operator pages with founding dates and current pricing, archived pages or dated reviews showing 12 or more months of operation, Toastmasters club or district fee pages.
- why it matters: SpeakX sells at ₹299 per month with a claimed 200,000 paying subscribers (02-signals.md#S01, S02) and free options exist (01-local-market.md#C15, C17), so the idea sells against cheaper and free alternatives.

### KM4
- dies unless: the graveyard check does not show a pattern of similar ventures failing for demand reasons.
- threshold: a documented search of at least 5 distinct queries (English, plus Hindi or Marathi terms) for shut-down or abandoned offline English-conversation clubs, paid language meetups or student paid-community startups in India; and, among any found, fewer than 2 whose stated or evidenced cause of death is low willingness to pay or poor retention. If 2 or more such failures are found, the idea dies unless the forge gives dated evidence of what is different.
- evidence that satisfies it: news, founder post-mortems, archived pages, Meetup group history (member counts and last event dates), and the search log itself.
- why it matters: the local scan found no failure data, which it states is absence of evidence only (01-local-market.md#C09).

### KM5
- dies unless: the reachable Pune student market is large enough that about 50 paying members is a small share of those with the need.
- threshold: a sourced count of college and university students in Pune (district or city) of at least 150,000, and a sourced indicator that at least 15% of Indian college students report low spoken-English confidence or a wish to improve it (national data is acceptable only if labelled national). Under these figures 50 payers would be under 0.5% of the students with the need in Pune.
- evidence that satisfies it: AISHE or Savitribai Phule Pune University enrolment tables for Pune; employability or English-proficiency surveys with named sample and year (for example ones published by an assessment firm).
- why it matters: no Pune student count was found (01-local-market.md#C28), and the retention of a small paid club depends on a large enough pool.
