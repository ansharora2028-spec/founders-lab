## Core identity
- customer: Pune college student (undergraduate or postgraduate, aged about 18-24, Pune city colleges), paying personally by UPI. Narrowed within the raw idea: students who already want to speak English more fluently (for placements, interviews or confidence) and are not enrolled in a paid spoken-English course. [ASSUMPTION: this is the segment most likely to pay; no Pune data on it exists]
- problem: Wanting to practise speaking English with other people in a low-pressure, in-person setting, without paying institute prices (₹4,000-15,000/month) and without the isolation of an app.
- revenue model: Monthly membership of ₹499 per student, paid by UPI, for access to weekend café conversation sessions.
- pivot declared: no

## How it works
1. Discovery: a student sees an Instagram reel/post or a WhatsApp group message from a college community, or a Luma/Meetup listing, for a "Saturday English conversation club" at a named Pune café.
2. Trial: the first session is a free or low-price trial (ASSUMPTION: free first session, to measure conversion). The student registers with a Google Form and pays ₹499 by UPI link for the month if they continue.
3. Session: about 90 minutes on a weekend morning/afternoon at a partner café. Groups of 8-12 (ASSUMPTION), seated at tables, with a printed topic card, one structured activity (e.g. role-play, debate, "describe and guess") and a short feedback round. Students order their own food and drink at the café.
4. Receipt: 2-4 sessions a month (ASSUMPTION), a WhatsApp group for between-session practice prompts, and a short written note on common mistakes after each session.
5. Founder, each week (10 hours, K2): about 3 hours running one session (more if two), 2 hours preparing topics/activities in Google Docs, 2 hours posting on Instagram and WhatsApp and replying to enquiries, 1 hour on payments and attendance in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets, UPI payment links), 2 hours on café relations and recruiting a student ambassador. The founder works the job outside working hours (K9), so only weekend sessions are possible. A freelance facilitator may be hired per session once more than one session per weekend runs (K4, within the ₹1,00,000 limit).
6. Tools: Instagram, WhatsApp Communities, Google Forms/Sheets, a UPI payment link or Razorpay-type link, Luma for listings.

## Need gap
- [C01] Paid in-person alternatives in Pune are listed at about ₹4,000-15,000 per month (Let's Talk English Academy ₹7,000-12,000; Veta ₹5,000-9,000; Engspeak ~₹4,000/month on a ₹12,000 three-month course). Sources are promotional or directory pages. [source: https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/english-speaking-classes/pune/]
- [C02] Private tutors on Superprof average ₹702 per hour in Pune. [source: https://www.superprof.co.in/lessons/english/pune/]
- [C03] Cheaper app substitute: SpeakX consumer plan is reported at ₹299/month; ₹499 is about 1.7 times that. (national) [source: https://inc42.com/buzz/exclusive-ms-dhoni-joins-edtech-startup-speakxs-cap-table/]
- [C04] Free substitutes exist in Pune: open Toastmasters clubs (e.g. Pune-West, Aundh) and a free language-exchange directory with a Pune page. Toastmasters dues were not found. [source: https://www.toastmasters.org/Find-a-Club/01534609-01534609]
- [C05] Falls short: institutes are structured courses and cost 8-30 times ₹499; apps lack human interaction according to comparison blogs written by competitors (weak evidence). (global) [source: https://www.talkdrill.com/blog/compare/best-english-speaking-apps/]
- [C06] Toastmasters is aimed at public speaking and adult members; a casual student-targeted conversation format with peers of the same age was not found in Pune. Absence of evidence in a search with poor coverage. [source: https://mylanguageexchange.com/city/Pune__India.asp]
- [C07] Why open now: no Pune paid café English club was found on Meetup, Luma or in search. This can indicate an open gap or absent demand; the evidence cannot distinguish them. [ASSUMPTION: gap or no demand; unresolved]
- [C08] Cafés in Pune host recurring community events (eChai socials in Baner, a Saturday coffee meetup on Luma), so the venue format is used locally for other purposes. [source: https://echai.ventures/pune/communities/recurring-meetup]

## How it makes money
- who pays, for what, how much, how often: the student pays ₹499 each month by UPI for access to that month's weekend sessions. Recurring monthly; cancellation by not renewing.
- other revenue (if any): none planned. Possible later (not modelled): café sponsorship or a per-head fee from the café, or a placement-interview-practice add-on. These are not part of the core revenue model.
- what one sale costs to deliver, and what drives the margin: variable cost per student is payment fees, printed materials, and any café venue fee or freelancer share per session. Margin is driven by group size per session (fixed cost of facilitator and venue is spread over attendees) and by renewal rate after month one. Whether cafés charge a hosting fee or accept a minimum-spend arrangement is unknown (C23 in 01-local-market.md).

## Narrowest wedge
One café, one weekend slot (Saturday morning), one group of up to 12 students, recruited from 2-3 colleges in one Pune neighbourhood (e.g. around Fergusson College Road/Deccan, where a Toastmasters club and many colleges are located; ASSUMPTION on clustering), priced at ₹499 for the first month including 2 sessions. The first paid transaction is a single UPI payment of ₹499. The wedge test is whether 10 or more students pay for a second month.
