## Core identity
- customer: an individual Pune tenant or owner-occupier of a 1-2 BHK flat who books a one-time deep clean (move-in, move-out, pre-festival), starting with one or two neighbourhoods (candidate: Wakad/Hinjewadi/Baner, where PG, co-living and tenant density is high per 01-local-market C19-C21)
- problem: the dominant app's reviewers report skipped areas, early departures, cancellation fees and no human support, so the buyer cannot rely on the deep clean they paid for
- revenue model: fixed price per booking paid by the customer (UPI, advance plus balance on photo sign-off); independent cleaning teams paid per job; the founder keeps the difference. No subscription, no commission marketplace.
- pivot declared: no

## How it works
1. Finding it: customer sees an Instagram reel or post (before/after photos), a Google Business Profile listing, or a message in a housing-society or tenant WhatsApp group, or is referred by a broker or friend.
2. Booking: customer messages on WhatsApp with flat size (1 BHK / 2 BHK), furnished or not, and area. The founder replies with a fixed quote from a rate card and a room-by-room checklist. Customer pays an advance by UPI.
3. Delivery: an independent two-person team (paid per job) attends. For each room the team uploads a before and after photo to a shared WhatsApp group. The team follows the checklist.
4. Sign-off: the founder reviews photos from the phone (ASSUMPTION: about 30 minutes per job) and sends the customer a summary. The customer pays the balance after sign-off. A missed item triggers a free re-clean by the same team at the team's cost.
5. Weekly founder work (about 10 hours): answer enquiries and quote (3 h), schedule teams and confirm slots (2 h), photo review and customer follow-up (3 h), content posting and group outreach (2 h). Tools: WhatsApp Business (free app), Google Sheets, UPI, Instagram, Google Business Profile. The founder does no cleaning and has a full-time job (K9), so jobs are scheduled on weekends or on days when a team can be left to work with the customer or a key-holder present.
- ASSUMPTION: two or three vetted teams can be recruited through personal contacts, cleaning-worker job boards or existing small cleaning outfits. No source for team availability or rate was found; this is the main supply risk.

## How it makes money
- who pays, for what, how much, how often: the household pays a fixed price per one-time deep clean. Candidate rate card, anchored to the Urban Company starting prices in C01-C02 and the per-sq-ft listings in C03-C04: about Rs 3,199-3,499 for 1 BHK (not undercutting the app, since the differentiator is the guarantee, not price). Frequency is once or twice a year per household, so repeat is low; referral and broker channels carry volume.
- other revenue (if any): add-ons such as sofa/mattress shampoo, kitchen chimney or wall cleaning (Ghare lists wall shampooing at Rs 1,500 for 1 BHK, C03) paid per job. Optional broker referral fee paid out, not income.
- what one sale costs to deliver, and what drives the margin: team pay per job (largest cost), cleaning consumables, travel, payment fees, and a re-clean reserve. Team pay evidence is indirect: Pune housekeeping jobs pay about Rs 15,000-20,000 a month (Rs 500-670 a day, search-engine arithmetic) [source: https://apna.co/job/pune/housekeeping-staff-292065227]. The Generator's team cost of about Rs 2,200 per job is an ASSUMPTION that fits a 2-person team plus supplies; a lower figure is plausible but unsourced for deep-clean freelancers. Margin is driven by team rate, re-clean rate and number of jobs per founder hour.
