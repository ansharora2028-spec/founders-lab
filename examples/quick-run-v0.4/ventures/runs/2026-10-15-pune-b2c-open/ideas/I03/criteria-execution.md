## Criteria

### KE1
- dies unless: the founder's total weekly hours (recruiting and vetting teams, booking, scheduling, photo review, customer support, re-clean disputes, marketing) stay at or under 10 hours per week (K2, HARD) at the volume the idea needs to cover its costs, and total cash at risk (ad spend, deposits, working-capital float for paying teams before the customer pays, re-clean reserve, damage cover, registrations) stays at or under ₹1,00,000 (K1, K5, HARD), with the founder never doing the cleaning.
- threshold: at 25 jobs a month, all founder tasks sum to ≤10 hours per week (the raw idea counts only about 30 minutes of photo review per job, which omits the other tasks); worst-case cash exposure in the first 3 months ≤₹1,00,000, with a stated ceiling on how much is paid to teams before collection.
- evidence that satisfies it: a task-by-task weekly hours table with sourced or clearly labelled time per task, and a cash-exposure table with Pune price quotes. A pilot log of at least 3 real jobs showing actual founder minutes per job would count as strong evidence.
- why it matters: the contract makes hours and capital HARD, and a service with daily coordination and disputes tends to take more hours than its headline task.

### KE2
- dies unless: at least 2 independent Pune cleaning teams are willing to work per job under the founder's photo-checklist and free-re-clean terms, at a per-job pay for a 1 BHK deep clean that leaves the founder ≥₹600 contribution per job at the planned price, and can cover the weekend and festival-season peaks.
- threshold: ≥2 named teams (or ≥3 quotes) with a stated per-job rate ≤₹2,300 for a 1 BHK deep clean (the raw idea assumes about ₹2,200 for the team); stated availability of at least 6 jobs a week; agreement to redo work unpaid when photos show a miss.
- evidence that satisfies it: written or recorded quotes from Pune cleaning teams or local providers, or published Pune team and contractor rates, plus the teams' terms on re-cleans.
- why it matters: the whole idea is a quality promise delivered by subcontractors the founder cannot supervise on site. If teams will not accept unpaid re-cleans, or quote above the margin, the promise has no supply behind it.

### KE3
- dies unless: the model of paying independent teams per job and collecting the customer's money is shown, from official text or a qualified source, to be allowed without requiring a licence, registration or a compliance cost that breaks the contract, and liability for damage, theft or injury on a customer's premises is covered within budget.
- threshold: total one-time legal and registration cost ≤₹10,000 and recurring compliance and insurance cost ≤₹1,500 a month; no requirement that the founder register as an aggregator or employer under the Social Security (Central) Rules 2026 (in force 2026-05-08) or the pending Maharashtra gig-worker bill (issued 2026-07-03, status pending), or if registration is required, a cost and timeline inside the threshold and within the 3-month limit to first revenue (K6, HARD); an insurance or damage-cover quote or a written damage policy that caps the founder's exposure.
- evidence that satisfies it: the official text or a government portal page on aggregator definitions and thresholds; the Maharashtra bill text and its status; Shop Act and GST position from an official PMC, Labour Department or GST portal page (GST threshold for services is ₹20 lakh, per the local-market file's consultancy-sourced claim); a priced liability or tool-cover insurance quote for home-service work in Pune.
- why it matters: the signals file lists S14 as a compliance risk for gig-style marketplaces and the generator itself did not verify the duty. The cannot-lose limit (K5) is ₹1,00,000, and one claim for broken furniture or a theft allegation could exceed the margin of many jobs.

### KE4
- dies unless: the conservative case (not the base case) leaves a positive contribution per job after team pay, consumables, payment fees, a realistic re-clean rate, damage claims and paid acquisition, and the idea reaches its first paid booking within 3 months of starting (K6, HARD).
- threshold: contribution per job ≥₹400 after a 15% re-clean rate (each re-clean costs a full team payout) and a ₹600 paid cost to acquire a first booking recovered within 2 jobs per customer, or a blended acquisition cost via referrals ≤₹300; ≥3 paid bookings within 90 days from the first day of marketing, from test channels (Instagram, WhatsApp, Google Business Profile, society groups).
- evidence that satisfies it: sourced Pune price points for 1 BHK deep cleans (competitors' quoted prices), team rates from KE2, a stated re-clean rate with a source or labelled assumption, and ad-cost data for Pune local-service leads; a landing page or WhatsApp test with ≥3 prepaid or confirmed bookings, or a sourced analogue conversion rate.
- why it matters: the raw idea says the first booking barely breaks even at about ₹700 per job before re-cleans and damages, so small errors on re-clean rate, ad cost or team pay turn margin negative.

### KE5
- dies unless: the offer survives incumbent response and does not depend on a platform that can change terms. Specifically, the planned fixed price is within a competitive range of what Urban Company, Snabbit and Pronto charge in Pune for a comparable 1 BHK deep clean, and the founder can acquire customers through channels it controls rather than through those marketplaces or one ad account alone.
- threshold: planned price ≤110% of the lowest Pune quote for a comparable 1 BHK deep clean from a funded app (otherwise the "stricter quality" claim must be tied to evidence that customers pay a premium); at least 2 working acquisition channels that do not need a marketplace listing; evidence of how the funded incumbents have responded to local rivals (copying photo checklists, guarantees or re-clean policies), or a stated reason they cannot.
- evidence that satisfies it: the live Pune price pages of Urban Company, Snabbit and Pronto (or their absence from Pune), their stated re-do or satisfaction guarantees, and the WhatsApp Business and Meta ad policy terms that apply to service ads and templates.
- why it matters: funded rivals (reported Snabbit ~$400M and Pronto ~$200M valuation talks, S07) can copy a guarantee at no extra cost, and the founder's moat is a promise that incumbents may already offer.
