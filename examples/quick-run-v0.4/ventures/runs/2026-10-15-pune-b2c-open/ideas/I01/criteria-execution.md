## Criteria
### KE1
- dies unless: the full service (intake on WhatsApp, pro-rata calculation sheet, refund letter in English and Marathi, helpline filing steps, marketing and support) can be delivered at the stated volume within 10 hours a week (K2, HARD) and at most ₹30,000 of the ₹1,00,000 capital (K1, HARD) before the first paid file, using only skills the user has (K3: spreadsheets, social media marketing, English/Hindi/Marathi) or can hire inside K1.
- threshold: total founder time at 15 files a month, including marketing, intake, support and follow-up questions, is ≤10 hours in every week (not an average); pre-revenue spend ≤₹30,000; no HARD constraint broken in the conservative case.
- evidence that satisfies it: a time-per-file breakdown (intake, calculation, letter, follow-up) that sums to ≤10 hours a week with marketing included; a costed list of tools and ads; evidence that drafting a refund letter and calculation needs no skill outside K3 (no legal qualification).
- why it matters: K1 and K2 are HARD; a service that quietly needs 15+ hours of back-and-forth per week fails the contract however good the margin looks.

### KE2
- dies unless: the activity of preparing refund letters and telling a student how to claim can lawfully be sold by a non-lawyer in India without breaching the Advocates Act, 1961, consumer-protection rules or a bar-council or consumer-forum restriction on paid claim preparation, and the user's liability if a file fails is limited and insurable or disclaimable within the budget.
- threshold: at least one authoritative source (statute text, bar council or court position, or a regulator page) showing that non-lawyer paid document preparation and procedural guidance of this kind is permitted, plus a stated liability limit (disclaimer, fee refund policy) costing ≤₹10,000 to set up; no registration or licence needed that the user does not hold (K8 and K5 respected).
- evidence that satisfies it: statute or court text on unauthorised legal practice and its limits; a comparable non-lawyer paid consumer-complaint service operating in India; a refund-policy design that caps loss at the ₹799 fee.
- why it matters: a legal-adjacent paid service risks a bar-council complaint or consumer-court action, and K5 says the user cannot lose more than ₹1,00,000.

### KE3
- dies unless: the service does not depend on a channel or counterparty that can switch it off at will, or a workable fallback exists: WhatsApp (Business terms on automated and bulk messaging), Instagram and aspirant groups (Telegram, WhatsApp) for acquisition, UPI collection, and the National Consumer Helpline or e-Daakhil portal as the claim route the product teaches.
- threshold: acquisition reaches ≥15 paying files a month through channels the founder can use without paying more than ₹150 of cautious CAC per sale (the generator's own placeholder) and without relying on posting inside groups whose rules ban paid promotion; the helpline or portal route is documented as open to a student filing on their own, with the current procedure cited.
- evidence that satisfies it: current WhatsApp Business and Instagram commercial policy pages; group-rule examples from MPSC/UPSC aspirant communities in Pune; the current NCH and e-Daakhil filing procedure; at least one measured conversion or reply-rate data point from a comparable Indian service.
- why it matters: the generator's plan rests on organic posts in groups the user does not own, and on a government route that can change; either failing leaves no sales path.

### KE4
- dies unless: the user can handle the incumbents' reaction and the data duty: coaching institutes (which print non-refundable registration terms) and free alternatives (Education Ministry guidelines, the free National Consumer Helpline, consumer-rights groups, free guides and lawyers' blogs) do not make a ₹799 paid file redundant, and handling students' fee receipts, ID and contact data complies with the DPDP Act and Rules 2025 as they apply to a sole proprietor.
- threshold: evidence of at least one comparable paid, non-free refund or complaint-drafting service in India that still sells while free routes exist; the DPDP duties applicable to the user (notice, consent, retention, breach handling) listed and costed at ≤₹5,000 a year, with the date they bind (the 02 file says main duties from about 2027-05-13).
- evidence that satisfies it: named paid competitors or analogues with pricing; the official DPDP Rules text or a reliable summary stating duties and exemptions; a retention and deletion policy for receipts.
- why it matters: if free routes already cover the need, or data duties and institute pushback make each file costlier than ₹799, the product has no durable position.

### KE5
- dies unless: the model survives success and the 3-month clock: the first paid file is received within 3 months (K6, HARD) from a standing start, and if volume rises, a competitor can copy the template without a defensible edge, and refund outcomes do not depend on a counterparty (institute) whose refusal the founder cannot influence.
- threshold: first paid file ≤90 days from launch with a launch plan that reaches the first 30 target students by day 30; the trigger event (leaving a course or a batch change) occurs often enough that ≥15 files a month is reachable in the Pune pool; at least one defensible asset (e.g. an institute-by-institute refund-terms database, a track record of successful refunds) can be built within the budget; and the offer states clearly what the student gets if the institute still refuses.
- evidence that satisfies it: a dated launch plan with numbers; any count or proxy for coaching dropouts or complaints in Pune (consumer-forum cases, helpline counts); an analysis of how a copyist would replicate the template and what the founder's edge is; the refund-guarantee terms.
- why it matters: K6 is HARD, the event that triggers a purchase is infrequent (the generator's own pre-check names this), and a template product is trivially copied the moment it works.
