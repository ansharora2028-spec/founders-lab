## Raw idea
### I01
- pitch: Refund-claim file for Pune coaching students who leave a course (MPSC/UPSC and similar), ₹799 per file
- customer: a student or parent in Pune who has paid a coaching institute and wants to leave or has been hit by a batch change, and is refused a refund
- problem: institutes list registration fees as non-refundable and refuse refunds, while Education Ministry guidelines and a Pune consumer forum support pro-rata refunds; the student does not know how to calculate or claim one
- revenue model: one-time fee of ₹799 per refund file (fee calculation sheet, refund letter in English or Marathi, steps to file on the National Consumer Helpline). No success fee, to stay clear of acting as a lawyer.
- how it works: A student finds the service through Marathi and English posts in aspirant groups and Instagram, and sends the fee receipt and the institute's terms over WhatsApp. The founder fills a spreadsheet template that works out the pro-rata amount, produces a letter the student can send, and explains how to file on the helpline (1915). The founder does not give legal advice or appear for the student, and says so up front.
- need gap: Today the student either accepts the loss, pays a lawyer (price not found; ASSUMPTION: several thousand rupees), or reads generic guides. Course fees are Rs 50,000-1,25,000 with Rs 5,000-10,000 non-refundable registration (D01, C22-C24). Refund refusal and batch-change complaints are recurring (C47, C48), and guidelines from January 2024 call for pro-rata refunds (C38). The gap is that no local, Marathi-language, done-for-you option was found, but absence in a search is weak evidence. The free national government coaching push (S10) may reduce new enrolments over time.
- how it makes money: Students pay ₹799 once, ASSUMPTION based on being about 1% of a ₹80,000 course. Delivery costs about 30-45 minutes of founder time and almost no cash (ASSUMPTION). About 15 files a month (ASSUMPTION, no volume data found) gives about ₹12,000; tool and ad costs of ₹2,000-3,000 a month (ASSUMPTION) would be covered by about 4 files.
- lens: unbundle
- signal ids: L01, D01, C38, C47, C48 (01); S10 (02)

## Market criteria
### KM1
- dies unless: individuals already pay third parties for consumer-refund or complaint-drafting help (demand evidence), at a price comparable to ₹799 per case.
- threshold: at least 3 named sellers in India (at least 1 in Pune or Maharashtra) publicly charge individuals a one-time fee of ₹500 or more for refund claims, legal-notice drafting or consumer-complaint filing, AND at least 2 of them show evidence of paying customers (for example 10 or more customer reviews each, or a stated case count). Any India consumer-grievance startup found to have shut down must have its cause of death stated; a shutdown attributed to individuals not paying for one-time grievance help counts against this criterion.
- evidence that satisfies it: seller pricing pages, lawyer or legal-services marketplace listings (LegalKart, LawRato, Vakilsearch type), and review counts, each opened and dated within the last 24 months.
- why it matters: a PASS needs existing spending on a similar or worse solution; the scouts found coaching fee prices (D01), not any spending on refund help.

### KM2
- dies unless: the number of coaching-refund disputes reachable in Pune is large enough to give 15 paid files a month (the idea's own cautious volume) at a realistic conversion.
- threshold: public data showing at least 2,000 education or coaching grievances per year in Maharashtra (National Consumer Helpline, state consumer commission data), or at least 50,000 paying coaching enrolments per year in Pune, so that 180 files a year is 9% or less of disputes or 0.4% or less of enrolments.
- evidence that satisfies it: consumerhelpline.gov.in sector and state dashboards, Maharashtra consumer commission statistics, Pune coaching enrolment figures from a government, industry or credible press source.
- why it matters: the scouts have no volume data (01 C47 and C48 are anecdotes and one Nagpur case); the 15 a month figure is an assumption.

### KM3
- dies unless: the free substitutes do not already cover the same job.
- threshold: no more than 1 free, Pune-accessible service or template (NCH 1915 portal, Grahak Panchayat Pune, legal-aid clinic, coaching-refund guide or YouTube video in English or Marathi) that provides both a pro-rata refund calculation and a ready-to-send refund letter; if 2 or more exist, the idea survives only if there is evidence that people still pay despite them (reviews, case counts or sales of comparable paid kits).
- evidence that satisfies it: a documented search of free resources with URLs, plus any evidence of paid uptake in the presence of free options.
- why it matters: the generator itself named free guides and the free consumer helpline as the most likely killer; "do nothing" and free templates are the main substitutes.

### KM4
- dies unless: the refund right is enforceable against the type of institutes the customer would be leaving, so a ₹799 file leads to money back often enough to be worth buying.
- threshold: at least 3 consumer commission or court orders dated within the last 36 months ordering a coaching institute to refund fees (full or pro-rata) despite a non-refundable clause, at least 1 from Maharashtra, AND the Ministry of Education guidelines of January 2024 cited in 01 C38 are confirmed to cover private coaching institutes in Maharashtra, with the scope stated.
- evidence that satisfies it: order texts or reputable legal-news reports with case names and dates; the guideline text on education.gov.in or a Maharashtra government source.
- why it matters: 01 C38 rests on a secondary wiki and C47 has no date; if refunds are rarely won, customers will not pay or will ask for money back.

### KM5
- dies unless: the target customer can be reached in Pune without paid distribution that exceeds the price.
- threshold: evidence of at least 3 Pune-based or Marathi-medium MPSC/UPSC aspirant communities (Telegram, Instagram, Facebook or YouTube channels) with at least 5,000 combined followers or members, AND at least 10 public posts or comments in the last 12 months complaining about coaching fees, refunds or batch changes.
- evidence that satisfies it: URLs of the communities with follower counts and dated complaint posts, found by searching in Marathi and English.
- why it matters: 01 found no usable r/pune or local community data and rated WhatsApp and Instagram channels only by assumption; the idea depends on being found at the moment of dispute.

## Execution criteria
### KE1
- dies unless: the full service (intake on WhatsApp, pro-rata calculation sheet, refund letter in English and Marathi, helpline filing steps, marketing and support) can be delivered at the stated volume within 10 hours a week (K2, HARD) and at most ₹30,000 of the ₹1,00,000 capital (K1, HARD) before the first paid file, using only skills the user has (K3: spreadsheets, social media marketing, English/Hindi/Marathi) or can hire inside K1.
- threshold: total founder time at 15 files a month, including marketing, intake, support and follow-up questions, is ≤10 hours in every week (not an average); pre-revenue spend ≤₹30,000; no HARD constraint broken in the conservative case.
- evidence that satisfies it: a time-per-file breakdown (intake, calculation, letter, follow-up) that sums to ≤10 hours a week with marketing included; a costed list of tools and ads; evidence that drafting a refund letter and calculation needs no skill outside K3 (no legal qualification).
- why it matters: K1 and K2 are HARD; a service that quietly needs 15+ hours of back-and-forth per week fails the contract however good the margin looks.

### KE2
- dies unless: the activity of preparing refund letters and telling a student how to claim can lawfully be sold by a non-lawyer in India without breaching the Advocates Act, 1961, consumer-protection rules or a bar-council or consumer-forum restriction on paid claim preparation, and the user's liability if a file fails is limited and insurable or disclaimable within the budget.
- threshold: at least one authoritative source (statute text, bar council or court position, or a regulator page) showing that non-lawyer paid document preparation and procedural guidance of this kind is permitted, plus a stated liability limit (disclaimer, fee refund policy) costing ≤₹10,000 to set up; no registration or licence needed that the user does not hold (K8 and K5 respected).
- evidence that satisfies it: statute or court text on unauthorised legal practice and its limits; a comparable non-lawyer paid consumer-complaint service operating in India; a refund-policy design that caps loss at the ₹799 fee.
- why it matters: a legal-adjacent paid service risks a bar-council complaint or consumer-court action, and K5 says the user cannot lose more than ₹1,00,000.

### KE3
- dies unless: the service does not depend on a channel or counterparty that can switch it off at will, or a workable fallback exists: WhatsApp (Business terms on automated and bulk messaging), Instagram and aspirant groups (Telegram, WhatsApp) for acquisition, UPI collection, and the National Consumer Helpline or e-Daakhil portal as the claim route the product teaches.
- threshold: acquisition reaches ≥15 paying files a month through channels the founder can use without paying more than ₹150 of cautious CAC per sale (the generator's own placeholder) and without relying on posting inside groups whose rules ban paid promotion; the helpline or portal route is documented as open to a student filing on their own, with the current procedure cited.
- evidence that satisfies it: current WhatsApp Business and Instagram commercial policy pages; group-rule examples from MPSC/UPSC aspirant communities in Pune; the current NCH and e-Daakhil filing procedure; at least one measured conversion or reply-rate data point from a comparable Indian service.
- why it matters: the generator's plan rests on organic posts in groups the user does not own, and on a government route that can change; either failing leaves no sales path.

### KE4
- dies unless: the user can handle the incumbents' reaction and the data duty: coaching institutes (which print non-refundable registration terms) and free alternatives (Education Ministry guidelines, the free National Consumer Helpline, consumer-rights groups, free guides and lawyers' blogs) do not make a ₹799 paid file redundant, and handling students' fee receipts, ID and contact data complies with the DPDP Act and Rules 2025 as they apply to a sole proprietor.
- threshold: evidence of at least one comparable paid, non-free refund or complaint-drafting service in India that still sells while free routes exist; the DPDP duties applicable to the user (notice, consent, retention, breach handling) listed and costed at ≤₹5,000 a year, with the date they bind (the 02 file says main duties from about 2027-05-13).
- evidence that satisfies it: named paid competitors or analogues with pricing; the official DPDP Rules text or a reliable summary stating duties and exemptions; a retention and deletion policy for receipts.
- why it matters: if free routes already cover the need, or data duties and institute pushback make each file costlier than ₹799, the product has no durable position.

### KE5
- dies unless: the model survives success and the 3-month clock: the first paid file is received within 3 months (K6, HARD) from a standing start, and if volume rises, a competitor can copy the template without a defensible edge, and refund outcomes do not depend on a counterparty (institute) whose refusal the founder cannot influence.
- threshold: first paid file ≤90 days from launch with a launch plan that reaches the first 30 target students by day 30; the trigger event (leaving a course or a batch change) occurs often enough that ≥15 files a month is reachable in the Pune pool; at least one defensible asset (e.g. an institute-by-institute refund-terms database, a track record of successful refunds) can be built within the budget; and the offer states clearly what the student gets if the institute still refuses.
- evidence that satisfies it: a dated launch plan with numbers; any count or proxy for coaching dropouts or complaints in Pune (consumer-forum cases, helpline counts); an analysis of how a copyist would replicate the template and what the founder's edge is; the refund-guarantee terms.
- why it matters: K6 is HARD, the event that triggers a purchase is infrequent (the generator's own pre-check names this), and a template product is trivially copied the moment it works.
